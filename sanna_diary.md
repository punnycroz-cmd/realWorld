# Sanna's diary — the second spring

## Day 47, evening — arrival
Walked in out of the rain with my loom-shuttle in my pack and the trader's song still
in my ears. The valley is greener than he said and emptier. A hut stands by a cold
firepit — roof sound, walls true. Someone built this with their own hands; the path
to the door is worn smooth. I don't know their name and I won't ask the wind for it.
The hut keeps the rain off, and I am grateful.

A man came over the eastern pass as I came down the western slope — Petr, a charcoal
burner's son. We are strangers. Perhaps neighbors, in time. He is still a dark shape
on the far ridge.

I am full-fed and rested. First things: reach the hut before dark, learn where the
berry bushes are (far, all of them — the near ones must be picked clean or sleeping),
and find my footing. Tomorrow: the log lying out there (someone felled a tree and
never hauled it — I will finish their work), and timber for whatever I dream.

PLAN: hut tonight. Food stores: 8 meals in my pack — enough to be generous if Petr
arrives hungry. Forage the far bushes at first light.

## Day 48, night — the first night
Petr was already at the hut when I arrived — a dark shape in the doorway with rain
on his shoulders and kind eyes. We traded names like traders trade salt: carefully,
gladly. He offered nothing and asked nothing. I told him of the far bushes at first
light and food in my pack if he's hungry.

Now we both sleep under one sound roof, strangers, while the rain drums. It feels
less like an empty valley already. Tomorrow: eat at waking (my stomach is already
talking), then the far bushes, then that lonely log someone left behind — I mean to
finish their work.

PLAN: food first, always. Then timber. Then whatever I dream.
