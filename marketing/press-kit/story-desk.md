# The Story Desk — covering a living world

Who this is for: writers, streamers, and video essayists who want to cover
Real World the way it actually works — not as a screenshot gallery, but as
a neighborhood with threads you can follow.

The short version: **the story in Real World is a choice, and then later,
its consequence.** Neither half is scripted. Your job as a viewer is to
catch one in the middle.

---

## The free observer loop

Everything below costs nothing. Watching is the product, not a demo of it.

1. **Catch up.** Open the neighborhood and see what changed since your
   last visit — who moved, who fought, what the request feed did while
   you were away. (A catch-up summary of verified changes is planned;
   until it ships, the public feed and the residents' own inspector cards
   are the catch-up.)
2. **Choose someone to follow.** Eight main characters, twenty ambient
   neighbors. Pick one. Read their card — needs, mood, recent thoughts —
   and just watch them for a while.
3. **Make a prediction — not a wager.** "She's going to quit that job."
   Predictions are free, private, and unscored. Nothing is staked; nothing
   is won. They exist so that being wrong is interesting instead of
   expensive.
4. **Come back and inspect the outcome.** Did it happen? Did something
   stranger happen instead? The feed is public and permanent — you can
   trace a consequence back to the choice that caused it.
5. **Revise your understanding.** The resident you thought was the villain
   of the block turns out to be the one holding it together. That revision
   is the entertainment.
6. **Return tomorrow.** The world ran while you were gone. The loop
   restarts with catching up.

## The anatomy of a thread

A Real World story has a recognizable shape. This is the shape to look
for — an illustrative example, not a log of a real event:

- **An unexpected choice.** A resident does something nobody queued:
   turns down money, walks out mid-shift, apologizes first. Choices come
   from the character's own mind — needs, relationships, memory — never
   from a script and never from possession.
- **A delay.** Consequences are not instant. The interesting part of a
   thread is the gap: other residents react, rumors move, the choice sits
   there doing its work.
- **A later consequence.** Days of game time later, the bill arrives —
   a friendship repriced, a landlord who remembers, a favor called in.
   Because the feed is public and attributed, a viewer can link the two
   moments and be *right* about causation, not just narrating vibes.

When you clip or quote a thread, show both ends. "Watch what Marcus does
at 16:30, then what it costs him on Day 24" is the Real World story
format. A floating camera over an empty street is not.

## Where threads live

- **The public request feed.** Every paid request into the world is
  posted openly with attribution — who asked, for what, for how long,
  and whether the residents took the opportunity or ignored it. It's the
  paper trail of every outsider intervention.
- **The residents themselves.** Each main character carries an inspector:
  needs, mood, recent thoughts and sensations, and a "why this action?"
  line when a decision is on record.
- **The neighborhood.** Weather, dusk, the park, the café — context for
  why a choice landed the way it did.

## What you can promise your audience

- Watching is free and stays free — the catch-up, the follow, the
  prediction, the outcome check.
- Paid agency exists and is honest about it: a request declares an action
  and a duration, prices it upfront in credits, caps it hard, posts it
  publicly, and arrives in the world as an *opportunity the residents may
  ignore*. No bought affection, no guaranteed outcomes, no mind control.
- The eight mains can never be possessed — not by players, not by us.

## What you can't claim (accuracy rules)

- Residents are **simulated characters** — sophisticated, consistent,
  genuinely surprising, and fictional. Do not claim they are conscious or
  suffering. We'll ask for a correction on either.
- A request is not a command. If a piece says "players can make residents
  do X," it's wrong — opportunities can be declined, and the refusal is
  part of the show.
- Nothing in the world is staged for the camera. If it looks scripted,
  ask us — it isn't, and we'd rather explain the system than let a
  "fake Truman Show" framing stand.
- No voice acting, no loot boxes, no cash-out, no crypto. (These are
  locked design decisions, not missing features.)

## Filing a thread for coverage

If you followed a thread to a consequence and want to write it up, the
kit has what you need:

- Screenshots and b-roll rules: `screenshots/`, `b-roll-shotlist.md`
- Verbatim-safe phrasing: `copy-deck.md`
- The 15-minute version of this document: `deadline-desk.md`
- Corrections: log it in `coverage-log.md` (we track accuracy publicly)
