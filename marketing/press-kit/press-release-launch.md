# [STUDIO NAME] announces *Real World*: a persistent AI "Truman Show" on a real San Francisco block — free to watch, paid to reach into

**[CITY], [DATE]** — *[STUDIO NAME]* today announced *Real World*, a
browser-based life simulation whose first neighborhood, **"The Mission,"**
recreates the streets around Dolores Park in San Francisco's Mission District
and fills them with twenty-eight fictional residents who live, work, feud, and
reconcile around the clock — whether anyone is watching or not.

Watching is free, always. The game's only paid layer is **agency**: viewers
who want to reach into the world file a request — an action plus an exact
duration, priced upfront in credits, hard-capped with no overrun, and posted
to a public feed everyone can read. Requests that expire in queue are
automatically refunded.

The cast's eight main characters run on full AI minds — and under a rule the
developers call the possession ban, **nobody can control them**: not players,
not the game's own owner. Their storylines stay authorial and their secrets
stay theirs to leak. Players who move into the neighborhood do it the hard
way — they create a character, rent in-game housing in game dollars, work a
job, and can climb the block's oldest ladder: tenant, owner, landlord.
