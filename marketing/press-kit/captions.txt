screenshots/v84-A.png       1440x900 (approx)
  The Mission block from overhead on an autumn afternoon (in-world
  Day 22): the Mudhaus Coffee dollhouse cutaway open on the block —
  the building ghosted, a real floor plan drawn inside the footprint,
  Jules tagged inside it, Marcus and Victor at the door. The Wire's
  rooftop camera feed composites live into the corner monitor with
  DIRECTOR chrome and a viewfinder strip reading lens, EV, compass,
  and height. The ground itself now reads lit vs. shaded — street cells
  carry the warm key only where the direct beam lands, skylit umbra
  where it doesn't. The HUD shown is the real spectator chrome of the
  development build.

screenshots/v84-B.png       1440x900 (approx)
  Street-level close view of Jules on the sidewalk with Priya and Dani
  mid-block: dressed storefronts and stoops, parked cars along the
  curb, leaves drifting — residents carrying name tags overhead, the
  rooftop-over-park monitor live in the corner. The pavement reads
  sunlit in the beam and open shade out of it.

screenshots/v84-C.png       1440x900 (approx)
  Dolores Park from overhead: crown-genome trees in rust and gold —
  every crown grown from a genome atlas and flipped per instance, so no
  two read alike — meadow drifts stippled across the lawns, picnic
  blankets on the grass, tennis courts holding the south edge,
  perimeter streets ringing the park — all under the renderer's
  sun-driven frame grade, contrast and exposure computed on the world
  plate before the optics pass.

screenshots/v84-D.png       1440x900 (approx)
  Director mode at street level: the thirds grid and center cross up on
  the composition guides (H key), REC cluster and DIRECTOR badge, the
  corner monitor's viewfinder strip reading 50mm, f2.0, EV+0.0, compass
  and height — dressed fronts and ridge-crested rooftops under a clean
  sky. The camera rig treats the neighborhood like a film set because,
  to a spectator, it is one.
