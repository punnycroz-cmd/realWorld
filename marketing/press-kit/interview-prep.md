# Interview Prep — spokesperson sheet (internal, not for distribution)

> Companion to `press-qa.md` (anticipated questions → approved answers). This
> sheet is for live interviews/podcasts/streams: the five things we always
> say, how to bridge away from bad framings, and the red lines that keep us
> honest. Speaker: [FOUNDER NAME — slot]. Keep answers under 45 seconds;
> land one of the five points, then stop talking.

## The five points (say these, in any order)

1. **Watch free, always.** The neighborhood runs 24/7 whether anyone pays.
   The show is the product, not a demo funnel.
2. **Agency, not access.** The only thing for sale is time-boxed influence:
   a request with a declared action, an exact duration, an upfront credit
   price, a hard cap — logged on a public feed everyone can read.
3. **The possession ban.** The eight main characters can never be controlled
   by any human — including the owner. That's the trust contract; it's why
   the drama means anything.
4. **A real block, fictional people.** Real Mission streets and Dolores
   Park; generated addresses that can't map to a real door; parody business
   names. Local enough to walk, fictional enough to be safe.
5. **No dark patterns.** No loot boxes, no cash-out, no credit trading, no
   crypto, no voice cloning. Credits are non-transferable on purpose.

## Bridges (question → where to take it)

- *"So it's like The Sims?"* → "The Sims is a dollhouse you own; this is a
  neighborhood you visit. Nobody owns the cast — that's point three."
- *"Is this an NFT/crypto thing?"* → "The opposite — no cash-out, no
  trading, credits can't leave the game. Money only flows in, for minutes
  of agency." (Never get pulled into web3 debates; restate point five.)
- *"Can trolls ruin it?"* → "Requests knock before they enter — automated
  screening, human review on gray zones, refunds on every deny, and the
  mains are untouchable by design."
- *"When is it done?"* → "A persistent world is never 'done' — that's the
  point. It launches when the watch layer is solid; everything else is
  additive."
- *"How is this different from Twitch Plays?"* → "Twitch Plays is crowd
  control of one thing. Here the crowd watches a world that resists —
  requests are priced, capped, screened, and the stars can't be seized."

## Red lines — never say these

- Never imply the mains can be possessed, nudged, or "guided" by anyone.
- Never quote prices as final — always "provisional until launch."
- Never promise voice, multiplayer possession, modding, or a release date
  beyond what's publicly stated.
- Never describe AI internals beyond "full AI minds for the mains,
  lightweight minds for ambients" — no model names, no prompts, no costs
  beyond "cents per character per day at current API prices."
- Never disparage competitors; the comparison sheet (`context.md`) is the
  ceiling of what we say about other games.
- Never give the founder quote for them — the slot stays empty until the
  owner writes it.

## If you don't know

"That's not decided yet — I'd rather tell you that than invent a feature."
Then bridge to a point you do know. Every honest "not yet" is cheaper than
one correction.
