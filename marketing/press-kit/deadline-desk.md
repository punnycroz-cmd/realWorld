## The three facts that carry the story

1. **Watching is free, always.** The whole neighborhood — 28 fictional
   residents, the public request feed, the drama — costs nothing to watch.
   Watching is the product, not a demo.
2. **Players pay for agency, not access.** A request declares an action
   and a duration upfront, prices it in credits, hits a hard cap, and
   posts to a public feed everyone can read. No loot boxes, no cash-out,
   no crypto.
3. **Nobody can possess the eight main characters.** Not players, not the
   game's owner. Their storylines stay authorial; their secrets stay
   theirs. That's the trust contract that makes the watching honest.

## The five mistakes to avoid

- **Don't call it a game you "play" in the usual sense.** The default
  relationship to the world is watching; paid requests are the exception,
  time-boxed and capped.
- **Don't say players can control or possess characters.** Nobody can —
  that's the point. Ambient neighbors can be *hired* for bounded,
  disclosed jobs; mains never.
- **Don't imply a real address.** Streets are real Mission geometry;
  every resident and house number is fictional and generated so it can
  never be a real door.
- **Don't promise voice acting, cash-out, or player-owned economies.**
  All three are deliberate cuts.
- **Don't describe pricing as final.** Credit pricing is provisional;
  say "planned" or "provisional," never a locked dollar figure.
