# Context sheet — where *Real World* sits

> For writers who need the "what is this thing like" paragraph. Every
> comparison below is one we think is *accurate*, including where we fall
> short. Third-party figures are from public reporting as of Sept 2026 —
> verify before you cite them; they're here for orientation, not as our
> claims. (Internal market study; sources on request.)

## The one-line framings we'd endorse

- **"A Truman Show you can visit"** — a real neighborhood on camera 24/7,
  except the stars are fictional AI residents and nobody can grab the
  controls.
- **"The Sims, but you're the audience, not the god"** — the eight main
  characters are unpossessable by design; you watch them like a serialized
  drama and pay only to *reach in*.
- **"Twitch Plays, with an economy attached"** — spectators collectively
  steer a world through public, paid, time-boxed requests.

## Framings we would not endorse

- ~~"AI generates the story"~~ — the cast, rules, and world are authored;
  the AI performs it. Every paid request is screened and publicly logged.
- ~~"A metaverse / Second Life successor"~~ — no user-built world, no
  creator cash-out, no land speculation for real money.
- ~~"An NFT/crypto game"~~ — flat no. Credits are non-transferable and
  never redeemable.

## Comparison table

| Touchstone | What it is | What *Real World* shares | The difference that matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Sims / InZOI / Paralives** | Single-player life sims; you control the household | Life-sim texture: jobs, rent, routines, relationships | You never control the mains. The product is *watching* a persistent cast, not piloting one. Sims 4 is free-to-play with a ~$140 DLC catalog; we sell agency by the minute, not content packs. |
| **GTA RP / FiveM servers** | Humans roleplay jobs and rent on shared servers | "Live a second life with a job and a lease" is proven mass-market; players already pay for priority and status | Our residents are AI, so the block runs when nobody's online — and the economy can't be pay-to-grief because requests are hard-capped and publicly attributed. |
| **Twitch Plays Pokémon** | 1.16M people collectively steered one character via chat | Proof a huge audience will watch collective agency | TPP had no economy; we charge for *declared, time-boxed* interventions, each logged on a public feed. |
| **Generative-agents research** (Stanford Smallville, AI Town) | AI villagers with memory and routines | The "watch AI villagers live their lives" fascination is validated | Those were demos, not products: no persistence contract, no audience, no revenue model. Ours is a designed show with an authored cast — emergence is paced, not prayed for. |
| **Second Life** | Persistent virtual world, land-as-subscription for 20+ years | Tenant→owner→landlord as the status ladder; land economics work | SL land is infinite and user-built; ours is one finite real neighborhood. And SL needed a licensed money transmitter for cash-out — we never allow cash-out. |
| **RimWorld / Dwarf Fortress** | Emergent colony sims | Design north star: a drama-*pacing* layer over a social substrate (memory, rumors, reputation), never scripted plots | They generate stories for one player's colony; we run one shared neighborhood everyone watches. |

## Honest weaknesses (we'd rather you hear them from us)

- **No public build yet.** Everything playable is a development build;
  screenshots carry that label and should keep it.
- **The sim is the hard part.** Paralives delayed half a year on Live Mode;
  InZOI shipped beautiful and retained poorly. Our bet — authored cast +
  social substrate + a drama pacer — is unproven at commercial scale.
- **Watchability is the whole funnel.** If the free spectator feed isn't
  entertaining with zero players online, nothing downstream works. We know
  it; the design leads with it.
- **Pricing is provisional.** All credit/subscription figures are marked
  provisional until launch; please say so if you cite them.

## Quick answers to the comparison questions

- *"So it's an MMO?"* — one shared persistent world, but you don't walk it
  as an avatar. You watch it, and your character lives in it like a local.
- *"So it's a stream?"* — a world that happens to be watchable. The feed
  is the serialization; the world is the show.
- *"So it's a God game?"* — no. Gods don't file requests with budgets and
  a public paper trail.
