Honest comparison — no strawmen

Not a better dollhouse. A different genre.

The Sims, inZOI, and Paralives are all variations on one idea: you get a household, and the world exists for it. Real World inverts that — one shared neighborhood exists whether you’re there or not, and the main cast can never be controlled by anyone. If you want a dollhouse, buy a dollhouse; they’re excellent. This page is for deciding if you want the other thing.

The real difference

Every other life sim is a power fantasy. This one is a place.

In The Sims, inZOI, and Paralives, the world pauses when you close it and exists to serve your characters. That’s a great fantasy — it’s just not the one we’re building. Real World’s neighborhood keeps living when you log off: shifts are worked, feuds continue, listings fill. You come back to find out what you missed, in the archive, same as everyone else.

The possession ban is the sharpest edge of that. The eight main residents have their own memories and secrets, and no request can steer them. You can share the block with them — rent a room, work a shift, become a regular — but you can’t puppet them. That limit is what makes their stories worth watching.

It also makes honesty load-bearing. Every paid action is a declared, time-boxed, publicly attributed request — priced upfront, hard-capped, and posted to the same feed everyone reads. The economy can’t pay out: credits never convert back to money, and game dollars stay in the world.

What we don’t have: photoreal graphics, a building mode, a character-creator slide-fest, or a $140 content catalog. One neighborhood, text-based characters, pixel-art streets. We’d rather be small and alive than big and sterile.

Who it's for

Pick your game honestly.

Play The Sims / Paralives if…

You want to build homes, run households, and tell your own stories with total control. That’s what they’re for, and they do it better than we ever will.

Play inZOI if…

You want photoreal visuals and a whole city to manage from above, with AI citizens as a feature of your sandbox.

Watch Real World if…

You want a place that isn’t yours — a neighborhood to follow like a show, a feed to read like a local paper, and the option to move in on the same rules as everyone else.
