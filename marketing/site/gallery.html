Real captures — development build

Nothing here is a render.

Every image below is a frame captured from the actual in-browser build — spectator HUD, placeholder interface, rough edges and all. When the build changes, this page changes. Click any shot for the full-resolution PNG.

Latest — art pass v83, the roofs got furnished

Every roof is a real place now.

Flat roofs stopped being painted-brown guesses: each building bakes a deterministic catalog of what's actually up there — decks, furniture, penthouse pop-ups with sun-keyed walls that throw their own shadows — and the street-level view projects the same catalog, so a rooftop reads the same from above and from the sidewalk. Pitched ridges gain widow's-walk cresting and hip-apex finials. And because the catalog lives in world meters, not pixels, a roof deck is an addressable place the simulation can use — not just decoration. Underneath: the v82 field-memoization pass (street render roughly halved), the v81 coverage grammar, the v80 sky-visibility shade field.

Art pass v81 — the rig learned coverage grammar

The camera shoots like a crew now.

The follow-cam got a director's vocabulary: it pans into a subject's look-room off smoothed lateral velocity, composes a two-shot when a second resident enters the conversation — the boom widens, amber partner brackets come up, a 2-SHOT flag lights — and gusts buffet the lens scaled by exposure and crosswind. A director's thirds grid and center cross ride on SF_CAM.guides (H key, on by default), and the viewfinder strip now reads EV, compass, and height. Underneath: the v80 sky-visibility shade field, the rooftop murals, the rebuilt lens.

Art pass v80 — the light learned what's in the way

Shade knows what the sky can see.

The ground now answers a real question — how much sky does this spot actually see? A visibility field is marched through the building masses and the tree crowns' real volumes, so building-scale shade pools on the park's east edge, crown-shadow dapple lands on the lawns, and every resident, car and street prop self-shadows against the same field and picks up skylight fill only where the sky is actually open. Underneath: the v79 rooftop murals and the wind-blown roofscape, the v76 crepuscular lanes, the rebuilt lens.

Art pass v76 — crepuscular lanes and silver linings

The clouds learned to give the light back.

Every cloud in the sky now does two honest things. Each dome carries a silver lining — a sun-facing rim arc, warmer on bright afternoons — and in the top view each cloud throws a crepuscular lane: a warm volumetric streak from its body down to the sun-gap it shades, so you can watch light land on the Dolores lawns between the shadows. In director mode the same rays track to the facades and die in a warm splash where the beam foot hits. Underneath: the v75 living walls and traveling gust field, the rebuilt lens, one weather-driven visibility number.

Art pass v75 — living walls and wildflower drifts

The fronts went green, and the wind learned to travel.

Look at the residential fronts at street level: ivy and bougainvillea strands climb the walls — sun-warmed magenta bracts where the light hits — and balcony gardens trail over their rails. In the park, poppy and lupine stipple drifts spread across the free grass cells like a meadow nobody planted. And the wind itself now moves: a gust field advects downwind across the block, so the crest sweeps through trees and leaning facades as a wave instead of everyone swaying to their own private beat.

Art pass v71 — the dollhouse cutaway

Inspect a resident indoors and the building opens.

Follow someone through a door and the roof doesn't just sit there — the building ghosts to a quarter of itself and a real floor plan draws inside the footprint: door-anchored rooms, floors and furniture that match the building's archetype, a sun patch through the sun-facing wall, lamp pools after dark. The people inside are still the same chibi residents, still tagged, still readable — the show just gets a closer set. Underneath it all the lens stays the rebuilt one: a single weather-driven visibility number instead of stacked effects, and crown-genome trees in full autumn turn, so no two read alike.

Art pass v65 — parklets and papel picado

The street learned to dress up.

Zoom in and the street keeps going: parklets in the parking lane, papel-picado garlands and porch flags that flutter on the same wind the weather reads, cast-iron grate wells under every street tree, water-meter covers set into the sidewalk cells. Underneath it all the facades bake to an impostor atlas — same painted fronts, roughly half the canvas work per frame, so the block stays smooth while twenty-eight lives run on top of it.

Art pass v61 — weather you can see coming

The sky keeps its own schedule.

Karl hangs over the block in patches at 16:30 while distant shower cells stand on the skyline — columns, anvils, and rain shafts that lean with the wind. The park shot is the re-framed overlook: the perimeter streets and two rows of facades finally ring the lawns, so Dolores reads as a park inside a neighborhood, not a lawn in the woods. And when Karl's layer is heavy enough, the pavement wets even without rain — the fog drips, and the sim counts it.

Art pass v59 — after the rain

The block dries off in real time.

A wet Mission afternoon: pavement still speckled damp, facades darkened where the wind drove the wet against them, a stringcourse catching the light at every floor line. Up top, the flat roofs hold ponding puddles between the duct runs and the festoon lights. Nothing here is a filter — wetness is a live weather value the whole renderer reads.

Art pass v55 — the September turn

The neighborhood knows what month it is.

Late September in the Mission: the ginkgos go gold first, other crowns follow in rust and wine, and a gust shakes loose a drizzle of leaves. In the park, mow stripes follow the lawn contours and the palms drop a frond or two. The leaf-turn is deterministic per tree and driven by the in-world month — a simulated season cue, not an art swap.

Art pass v54 — the facade pass

The storefronts got faces.

Same Day-22 afternoon, one art pass later. Facades now carry painted windows, cornices, and signage — the block reads like the Mission, not a grid.

Art pass v53 — street furniture & lawn life

The block today.

Autumn, Day 22, late afternoon. Karl's marine layer is at the edges and the leaves are doing their thing.
