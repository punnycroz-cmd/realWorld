The cast

Twenty-eight people. One block. No script.

Everyone on this page is a fictional resident of a real neighborhood — the streets, the park, and the skyline around Dolores Park are real San Francisco. The eight mains run on full AI minds; the twenty neighbors run on schedules and reflexes. All of them keep living whether you're watching or not.

The eight

Main characters — public profiles.

What you'd learn about them by standing at the counter at Mudhaus for a week. Everything deeper — their secrets, their private fears, what's actually going on — stays theirs. Not even the people who run this world get to read it out of them, and nobody can possess them: not players, not us. That's the rule the whole show is built on.

Marisol “Mars” Delgado, 29

Manages Mudhaus Coffee on 24th St. Opens at six, knows every regular's order, and walks the block each afternoon — mail to Carmen, a word with Tomás, a lap around the park. Nobody asked her to check on everyone. She does it anyway.

Find her: behind the counter at Mudhaus, or doing her rounds.

Jules Park, 26

Newest barista at Mudhaus. New to the city — three years of Portland coffee, then a breakup and a rent spike pushed them south. Runs Dolores Park's north path most mornings; evenings, sketches the block in a notebook they guard closely.

Find them: training shifts at Mudhaus; renting a room on Guerrero St.

Dani Reyes, 24

Barista and Mudhaus's unofficial art department — chalkboards, window paint, the doodles on cup sleeves that regulars collect. Art-school dropout from Daly City; draws the neighborhood with merciless honesty.

Find her: six days a week on 24th St; somewhere else she doesn't talk about, nights.

Priya Raman, 31

RN at SF General — three twelve-hour shifts a week. Mudhaus is her decompression chamber: same seat, same oat latte, same nod to Marisol. Shares the top-floor flat at 750 Guerrero with Marcus.

Find her: post-shift at Mudhaus, or on the stairs at 750.

Marcus Bell, 34

Bike courier for the Flying Pannier co-op. Oakland native, ex funk-band drummer — still plays the Thursday jam circle by the park. Knows every porch on the block and most of the gossip that doesn't go through Marisol.

Find him: on a bike, everywhere; Thursdays at the drum circle.

Carmen Echeverría, 74

Retired seamstress; 744 Guerrero since 1989. Sewed for a Mission garment shop for thirty years, still takes in hemming by word of mouth. Mornings on the stoop with cafecito; afternoons under the same palm in Dolores Park. Has outlasted four landlords' worth of neighbors.

Find her: the stoop, the park, the front room with the sewing machine.

Victor Auerbach, 58

Third-generation owner of Auerbach Hardware on Mission St — and the landlord of two Victorians on Guerrero facing the park. Lives alone above the shop. Knows every pipe in both buildings by name; does the Tuesday repairs himself rather than pay a plumber.

Find him: behind the hardware counter, 9 to 6.

Tomás Herrera, 36

Lead cook at Taqueria El Farolote, Mission at 24th — fifteen years up the line from dishwasher. Days he walks the supplier loop pricing equipment; the 3 p.m. coffee at Mudhaus is the only fixed point of his afternoons.

Find him: on the line five nights a week; 3 p.m. at the Mudhaus counter.

The places

Real streets. Invented storefronts.

The stage is real geography — Dolores Park, Clarion Alley, Mission High, SF General, the library. The businesses are all fictional parody names, on purpose: they look like the Mission because they're meant to, and no real shop gets dragged into the fiction.

Same trick with addresses: real street names, generated house numbers that sit above the real block maximum, so a door in the game can never be a real household's door.

On the block

  • Mudhaus Coffee — the cast's nerve center on 24th
  • Taqueria El Farolote — late-night line, Mission at 24th
  • Auerbach Hardware — Victor's family store, Mission St
  • Dolores Perk — café tables at the park's edge
  • Buy-Rite Market & Creamery — groceries, and the line
  • Baguette About It Bakery — ovens on before dawn
  • The 600 Club — the bar the nights end at
  • Il Delfino — the date-night dinner spot
  • Dandy Lion Chocolate Co. — the treat stop
  • Needlepointe Tattoo, Folsom Auto & Sons, Bloom & Doom Flowers, Malik's Mini Mart, Frutería Las Palmas — the rest of the streetscape

And two tables with no door of their own: Calle Justa Workers' Table (Thu evenings) and The Rent Table (Wed evenings, library community room) — free, volunteer-run drop-ins for when work or housing goes wrong.

What this page doesn't tell you

Their secrets stay sealed — that's the product.

Every main character carries a private life the public profile will never print: things they're hiding, things they haven't admitted to themselves. Possession briefings redact all of it; even playing a character you hired never unlocks anyone else's secrets. You find out what the block is really about the way a viewer does — by watching long enough to notice.
