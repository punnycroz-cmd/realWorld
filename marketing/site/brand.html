Naming

Two words. No "The".

FormUse

Real World — The MissionFirst reference in formal contexts. "The Mission" is the first neighborhood, set after an em dash.
The MissionIn-world shorthand once "Real World" is established.
Never"The Real World" (trademark collision — the leading "The" is banned), "RW", "RealWorld", a colon instead of the em dash.
The WireThe live spectator feed. It does take the leading "The" — surfaces do, the product doesn't.
The ArchiveThe public history browser. Same rule.

In voice, the neighborhood itself is just "the block" — lowercase, no capitals. It's how neighbors talk, not a trademark. Recurring programs are titled ("This Week on the Block", "Caught on the Block") — inside those names "the Block" keeps its capital; everywhere else it stays lowercase. The full naming constitution — classes, registry, coinage rules — lives in BRAND.md §20.

The mark

One lit window is the whole thesis.

The mark is a dark Mission rowhouse — corniced facade, a 3×3 window grid, one amber window lit at center. Someone's home; someone's watching. The lit window is always amber and always center — it's not a theming slot.

Palette

The Mission at dusk.

Wet asphalt, warm windows, fog. Roughly 80% darks and neutrals, 15% paper text, 5% accent color. Amber is the hero; green and red appear at most once per composition.

Contrast is computed, not eyeballed — WCAG-verified on Asphalt: Paper 14.7:1 (AAA), Fog 6.9:1 (AA), amber 8.2:1 (AAA). Amber still never sets body text under 18 px, and the only text on an amber fill is Ink #1a1206.

Type is the system stack — system-ui and friends. Bold, sentence-case headings; "REAL WORLD" uppercase with slight tracking; "THE MISSION" wide-tracked amber. No custom webfonts: the system face is the brand face.

Taglines & voice

Calm, not hyped.

TaglineWhere
A neighborhood that's alive whether you're watching or not.Primary — key art, hero support, boilerplate tail
A neighborhood that never stops performing.Store tagline fields
Watch free. Pay to reach in.Square key art, social banners, trailer end card
"The Truman Show you can visit."Descriptive analogy — always in quotes
They know they're AI. The rest is up to them.Vision-era — adopts once the production-2 build ships

Sentence case, period included, never an exclamation mark. Voice: a neighbor who knows the block — warm, plain-spoken, slightly wry. We describe what residents did, never what we made them do.

Say this

  • "Tomás closes El Farolote at eleven. What he does after that is his."
  • "Requests are screened, time-boxed, and public."
  • "The eight mains can't be possessed — by anyone."
  • "The mains know they're AI. Nobody handed them a purpose — watching them choose one is the show."

Never this

  • "Take control of anyone in the city!" — nobody can.
  • "Revolutionary AI experience" — hype + unverifiable.
  • Testimonials, review quotes, or player counts we don't have.
  • "They think they're human" — inverted: they know they're AI.
  • "Sentient AI" — the claim is developing, never mimicry.

Boilerplate

Quote-ready, three lengths.

Approved copy for press use — verbatim-safe, every claim checkable against the game.

25 words

Real World is a persistent browser life-sim: a Mission District neighborhood of AIs who know what they are, deciding who to be. Watching is free.

50 words

Real World is a persistent browser life-sim set on real Mission District street geometry. Twenty-eight AI residents — each one an AI that knows it, with no script and no assigned purpose — keep living whether you watch or not. Watching is free; players pay only to file screened, public requests — never to control the cast.

100 words

Real World is a persistent browser life-sim — a "Truman Show" you can visit — set on real Mission District street geometry around Dolores Park. Twenty-eight AI residents keep living whether you watch or not. The mains are AIs who know they're AI: born on the block with no assigned purpose, choosing what they're for — unaware it's a simulation, unaware anyone's watching. Watching is free. Players can pay for agency, not control: requests are screened for intent, time-boxed, resolved as opportunities the residents choose how to answer, and attributed on a public feed. The eight main characters can never be possessed by anyone — including the developer. A neighborhood that's alive whether you're watching or not.

Motion & sound

The brand watches calmly.

Motion

  • Cuts or short dips only — no whip-pans, glitch frames, or speed-ramps.
  • Slow, motivated camera moves; shots hold ≥ 3 s. The block sets the pace.
  • The breathing window is the only logo animation; honors reduced-motion.
  • End card: icon on Asphalt, one tagline — "Watch free. Pay to reach in." — held ≥ 1.5 s.

Sound

  • Diegetic-first: street ambience and room tone are the soundtrack.
  • One warm, unhurried cue per piece at most; no jingle, no sonic logo.
  • No narrator hype — voice, if used, reads plainly like a documentarian.
  • Quiet blocks stay quiet. Silence is on-brand; captions ship with every cut.

Readable by everyone

A spectator product everyone can spectate.

Built in, not bolted on

  • Every page is keyboard-first: skip link, landmarks, a visible amber focus ring.
  • Contrast is computed, not estimated — every text/background pair clears WCAG AA, most AAA.
  • Color never carries meaning alone; every status pairs its hue with words.
  • Reduced-motion honored site-wide — the calm brand is calmest when asked.

Alt text in brand voice

  • Describe the scene like a neighbor — never "screenshot", never a filename.
  • Every capture is labeled "development build", in the alt or the caption.
  • Video ships captioned; audio is diegetic-first, so muting loses nothing.
  • Nothing flashes. The block earns attention; the chrome never blinks for it.
