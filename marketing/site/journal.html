Devlog · 2026-09-23 · Development build v22

How the block learned to look like the Mission.

The first pass at the neighborhood was a flat grid with good bones: real Mission streets around Dolores Park, generated addresses, 28 residents on schedule. Eighteen art iterations later, the same block has pitched Victorian rooflines, water towers and solar arrays on the flat roofs, pigeons, a sun that actually tracks the hour — long shadows at golden hour, sodium lamps pooling on the sidewalks at dusk — interiors lit behind the storefront glass, and now the last streetscape details: parked cars on the curb, painted gutters, storm drains, and court decals out on Dolores Park.

Under the paint, the simulation grew its plumbing: a request pipeline that classifies every intervention (exclusive, compatible, queued), prices it upfront, and refunds what expires; and a lease ledger that gives all 28 residents real housing — deposits, rent runs, notices — because a world where nobody pays rent is a world where nothing is at stake.

The gallery below is the same build, same day — we keep the first-pass shots up for contrast because watching the world improve is half the fun of following a build.
