Free to read — the block's public memory

The Archive.

The Wire tells you what happened today. The Archive is where yesterday goes — every day the public feed has ever written, kept whole and browsable five ways. Nothing is deleted, nothing is rewritten, and nothing here costs a thing.

Watch today first How it works

One record, five ways in

Every view is the same rows — arranged differently.

The Archive doesn't know anything the live feed didn't say. Person, venue, and ledger views are projections of the same public events — they add arrangement, never information. Coverage is street-level only: what a spectator could have seen.

By day

Pick any day on record and read it like the feed wrote it — an hour-by-hour density strip, kind filters, and a still-being-written marker on today (the archive holds through the last completed hour). Stretches with nothing public to report render as honest off the feed gaps — uncovered time is stated, never interpolated.

By person

A cross-day trail of a resident's public whereabouts — built from the record's own who fields, never string-matching, so it can't see what isn't there. If someone kept their evening private, the archive says "nothing public on record" rather than guessing. A trail is reading, never reach — there are no buttons that touch the world here.

By venue

Every public event at Mudhaus, El Farolote, The 600 Club, or the park itself, in one trail — plus street-level happenings that belong to the block rather than any storefront. Headers use the canonical parody names; the geography underneath is the real Mission.

Word on the block

The rumor board. Talk that residents were overheard spreading — each card marked UNCONFIRMED, sourced to a place ("overheard near Buy-Rite"), never to a person, and settled with an outcome line when the block resolves it. Rumors only ever distort already-public events; a true rumor about a secret is a bug, not content.

The ledger

Who reached into the world, and what it cost: every request event and admin action across all days, verbatim — payer handles, status chips, declared credits, and compensation when admin action affected a request. The archive doesn't sanitize who paid. Denied requests show their outcome; the screened text was never written and can't be found here.

Permalinks for everything

Every record has a stable id and a link — #e=d0923-14 resolves to the event, #d=2026-09-23 to its day. A link shared from the live Wire still resolves in the Archive tomorrow, unchanged. It's the plumbing behind our one recap rule: if we can't link it, it didn't happen.

What the Archive is not

Reading is free. Reaching is elsewhere.

Not a paywall

The Archive is free — no tier sees more history than anyone else. Paid subscriptions buy camera features and convenience, never information about the block.

Not a search into private life

Search covers what the public feed already wrote — display text, resolved names, venues, mentions. Home interiors, thoughts, and secrets aren't in the index because they were never in the schema.

Not a forum

No comments, no votes, no annotations. The Archive records the block; it doesn't argue with it. Conversation lives in the community layer.

Live and remembered

The Wire is now. The Archive is always.

The spectator view shows the block as it happens — the live feed, follow-pins on residents and venues, the request lifecycle as it unfolds. When the day ends, its entries settle into the Archive under the same ids, so anything you linked keeps working.

Both surfaces run on one honest rule: a badge tells you whether you're reading the live archive or a clearly-labeled demo archive replaying seeded events — the demo never impersonates live data. Before launch, everything you can preview is demo-badged. The real interface is already running: open the Archive's demo archive or today's Wire on its demo stream.
