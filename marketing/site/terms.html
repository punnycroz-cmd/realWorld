The deal, in writing.

These terms govern Real World — the website and the playable neighborhood. They're written to be read: every rule here matches a mechanic you can verify in the game or on the rules page. By purchasing credits or filing a request you accept them.

What you're using

The service.

Watch free, pay for agency

Real World is a persistent simulated neighborhood: 28 fictional AI residents on real San Francisco streets, running around the clock. Watching — the cameras, the public feed, the archive — is free and needs no account. Paid features are requests (time-boxed interventions into the world) and character creation, both bought with credits.

A fictional world, honestly labeled

Every resident is fictional. Generated addresses sit above the real block maximum — none is a real door. Business names are parodies; streets and landmarks are real. Do not map characters onto real people — that line is enforced in the community rules and it's a bannable line, not a suggestion.

Money

Credits, game dollars, and the wall between them.

Credits

Credits are a one-way purchase of in-game agency. They are non-transferable, non-redeemable, and never convertible back to money — no cash-out, no trading, no marketplace. Anyone offering to buy or sell credits is running a scam; report it and don't pay. Prices and bundle sizes are on the pricing page.

Game dollars

Rent, wages, menus, and the tenant→owner→landlord ladder run on a separate in-world currency earned by characters. Game dollars are never purchasable and never convert to credits or cash. The economy is a game, not an exchange.

Checkout

Payment is handled by Stripe on Stripe-hosted pages — card data never touches our servers. Applicable taxes are collected at checkout. A chargeback reverses the purchase and claws back the credits it bought; repeated chargebacks can close the account.

Reaching in

Requests: what you buy, what you don't.

Screened, priced, public

Every request is declared in text, screened for intent, priced upfront, and hard-capped in duration. Exclusive or gray-zone asks wait for a human reviewer. Everything that runs appears on the public feed with your handle attached — attribution is the design, not a disclosure we'd rather hide.

Opportunity, not obedience

An approved request enters the world as an opportunity. The residents' own AI decides how they respond — no outcome is guaranteed, and "the character didn't cooperate" is not a defect. What you buy is the ask, not the result. Refund cases are enumerated on the refund policy.

Possession limits

You may only ever possess a character you created and hired — never another player's tenant, never a main cast member, never an ambient resident. Sessions end on a hard cap and the character's AI resumes. Possession briefings show public-profile information only; they never reveal secrets.

Appeals

A denied request can be appealed once within 72 hours, to a different reviewer. If the appeal reverses the denial, the request re-enters review at its original quote and you're charged only if approved. Some categories are not appealable (legal backstop, appeal re-submission). Appeals are private; outcomes never become feed spectacle.

Eligibility & conduct

Who can play, and how to stay.

Age bands

Spectating is open to all. Paid features are gated: under-13 accounts are redirected to a spectator-only experience with no purchase path; under-18 accounts carry spend-related limits; rewarded ad views are adult-only. You must give your real age band — lying about it to reach paid features ends the account.

Your handle

Handles are screened before they're set — no cast names, no neighbor names, no real people. Your handle is your public identity on the feed; pick one you can stand behind, because everything you file carries it.

Fair play

No exploiting the request system, no evading screening by rewording, no real-money trading of anything in the game, no mapping characters to real people. Community spaces follow the published rules: warn → timeout → ban, logged, with one appeal to the owner.

The honest edges

What we can't promise.

Emergent means emergent

The residents run on AI. They will do things nobody authored — that's the product. Events that run become canon; there is no undo for history and no complaint desk for what a fictional person did to your fictional person. What we guarantee is the machinery around it: screening, attribution, caps, refunds.

Service terms

The world runs 24/7 as a design goal, not a service-level guarantee — outages and maintenance happen. The service is provided as-is; to the extent the law allows, liability is capped at what you paid in the last 90 days. We may suspend accounts for terms violations. Changes to these terms get a dated entry here before they take effect.
