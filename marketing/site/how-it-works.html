How it works

Three ways in. One of them is free.

Real World is designed to be worth watching before it's worth playing. Start as a spectator, buy a moment of agency when curiosity wins, and move in for good if the block gets its hooks in you.

Step 1 — free, forever

Watch the neighborhood.

Open the block in a browser and look in. Pick a resident and follow their day — the café open at 6 a.m., the park loop at lunch, the stoop conversations in the evening. Switch between the overhead map, a street-level follow camera, and a director view that treats the block like a film set.

The public request feed scrolls alongside: every request other viewers have filed, every admin action, every refund. It's part transparency log, part serialized drama.

What you'll actually see

  • 8 main characters living full days on AI minds — jobs, errands, friendships, secrets.
  • 20 ambient neighbors keeping the sidewalks honest.
  • Weather, time of day, and the week's consequences accumulating.
  • Other players' requests landing in the world — attributed, priced, public.

Observation never costs credits and never will. Watching is the product's free core, not a trial.

Step 2 — pay for moments, not access

Get credits, then spend them on a request.

Two ways to get credits

Buy them in packs, or earn them by watching opt-in rewarded ads — capped per day, so nobody farms the economy. Credits are the only thing real money buys, and they buy agency only: requests, world changes, character creation.

Anatomy of a request

Every request declares what and for how long before you pay: "possess my character for 30 minutes," "rain over the park for two hours." The price is shown upfront, scaled to duration, and capped hard — when time runs out, control hands back to the AI gracefully.

What happens after you file

Classified automatically

Exclusive requests lock a resource (a character, the weather). Compatible ones run alongside others — two players steering two different characters through the same scene is allowed, and it's the best version of multiplayer here. Everything else is queued.

Fairly ordered

First-come-first-served, with per-player and global cooldowns. Contested resources get surge pricing. No auctions — priority can't be bought past the queue.

Publicly logged

Your request posts to the feed everyone reads. If a queued request expires before it activates, credits are refunded automatically. If an admin override cancels it, same thing.

One rule above all: requests can never target the eight main characters for possession — and can't target anyone for harm, humiliation, or property destruction. Contested or abusive requests are denied before they run.

Step 3 — join the cast

Create a character and move in.

Creating a character costs credits and requires renting in-game housing with game dollars — new arrivals pay rent like everyone else. Your character is the only one you may ever possess: nobody else's tenant, nobody's cast member, never the landlord.

Possession never reveals secrets. Your briefing holds a public profile, surface relationships, and the daily routine — every drama seed, yours and everyone else's, stays redacted. If you want to know what your neighbor is hiding, find out the way viewers do: watch.

Life on the inside

  • Hold a job and earn game dollars — rent, groceries, and transit don't pay themselves.
  • Log off and your character drops to quiet lightweight AI; log back in and the full mind wakes up.
  • Save toward a deed: buying a place adds mortgage, property tax, and HOA — the real-life costs, as gameplay.
  • Earn the reputation and the standing to rent out a second unit. Landlord is a status you reach, not one you buy.

The whole loop on one line

Watch a living street for free → spend a little on a time-boxed request and watch it resolve in the public feed → move in, rent a room, work a job → climb to owner and, if you earn it, landlord.
