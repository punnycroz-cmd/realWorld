Community spaces open at launch

The neighborhood watch.

A world this watchable deserves an audience that talks back. Real World's community is built around the same thing the game is: a public feed of everything anyone does to the block, recapped weekly, argued about daily.

The programming

A week on the block.

The community runs on a fixed weekly grid — small on purpose. A weekly recap that never misses beats a daily cadence that dies in week three, and a quiet week gets reported as a quiet week.

WhenWhatWhere
Sundays 18:00 PTThis Week on the Block — the recap. Which requests ran, which expired and refunded, what the residents did with it. Only real feed events.#announcements + the Journal
Sundays 18:05 PTOpen-rumor thread — one reply thread under the recap for what's still unconfirmed. If the recap has no open rumor, the thread simply doesn't run; we never invent one.#the-block
Wednesdays 19:00 PTRequest clinic — a walk through one or two filed requests end-to-end: the text, the classification, the outcome. How newcomers learn what "compatible" actually means.#help-requests
First Saturday, monthly 18:00 PTTown hall — one-hour text AMA. Roadmap questions get honest "don't know / not decided" answers; pricing answers match the pricing page verbatim.#the-block
When the feed says soWatch parties — big requests (a storm over Dolores Park, a rooftop set) get a coordinated viewing, with a voice channel for whoever wants it.#watch-party + #rooftop
Daily, as it happensFeed mirrors — notable public request-feed entries, mirrored and discussed. Capped; the feed itself is always the full record.#the-feed (read-only)

The space

Eight channels. That's the whole server.

A quiet forty-channel server reads dead; a busy handful reads alive. This is the full channel map — the server itself is created on launch day and verified against this blueprint before the invite link goes live here.

#the-block

The living room. General chat — who did what, what's still unconfirmed, whether Marisol meant that. Rules and the standing feedback asks stay pinned.

#the-feed

Read-only mirror of notable request-feed entries. Watching who is trying to do what is half the show — discussion happens in #the-block.

#watch-party + #rooftop

Coordinating simultaneous viewing when a big request lands. #rooftop is the voice channel; listening in is as welcome as talking.

#help-requests

"How do I file a request" and "what does compatible mean" support. How-it-works and pricing links pinned.

#feedback

Bugs and suggestions, triaged weekly and routed to the build. When a fix traces back to something you reported, the recap says so.

#announcements

Posts from the team only. The weekly recap lives here first, then on the Journal and socials.

#clips

Your captures of the feed and the block — screenshots, clips, the moment you were watching when it happened. The best of the community's own eyes, with credit.

The retention engine

What a recap actually contains.

Every Sunday's issue is assembled from the same public archive anyone can browse — never from our imagination. The shape:

The headline

The week lead: a player-called weather event, a notable request outcome, or the biggest crowd beat — whichever the feed says was biggest.

Request beats

Every request that ran, attributed by the name on the feed. Expired and refunded requests are counted in the open — the attempts are part of the story.

Resident beats

What the eight main characters did with their week — from their public behavior only. Secrets and private drama stay sealed; that's the contract that makes the show real.

The numbers + an open rumor

Request counts by outcome, straight from the ledger — and one unconfirmed thread the neighborhood is still arguing about, labeled unconfirmed.

Back issues will live on the Journal; the raw record behind every issue is the public Archive, free to read always.

From watcher to resident

The ladder, rung by rung.

1

Lurk

Watch the block, read the recaps, follow the feed. No account, no email, no purchase — the community is as free to observe as the world is.

2

Join

Community channels open on launch day. Accept the house rules, pick up the cosmetic resident role, find a thread worth arguing about.

@regular Stick around and the block notices — a recognition role, never for sale. Roster opens with the server; names appear with consent only.

3

Reach in

File your first request — a small, compatible one costs cents — or create a character and move in. When you do, the feed shows your name, and the recap might too.

4

Bring someone

Clip the moment you were watching for, post it in #clips, and send a friend the feed. Watching is free — the best referral pitch we have.

House rules

How the community stays honest.

The characters are fiction

All 28 residents are fictional people on real streets. Addresses are generated above the real block maximum — they can never be a real household's door. Don't try to map fiction onto real people anyway; that's the one hard line.

Requests are screened, not staged

Every request passes intent screening; exclusive or gray-zone requests get human review before they run. But nobody — including us — controls how a character's AI mind renders an approved request. The community discusses what happened, never a script.

No credit trading

Credits are non-transferable and non-redeemable — anyone offering to buy or sell them is running a scam, and it's an instant ban in our spaces.

Feedback goes somewhere

Bug reports and suggestions get triaged weekly and routed to the build. When a fix traces back to the community, the recap says so.

The full rules — what requests can't ask for, how review works, escalation, appeals — live on the rules & safety page.

Doors open with the world.

The community launches the day the neighborhood does — not before, because a community needs something real to watch. Until then, the site is the whole story: honest, complete, and updated as the build changes.
