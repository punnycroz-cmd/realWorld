### C1 — Marisol "Mars" Delgado

> Marisol opens the café at six. She knows your order, your landlord
> problem, and — though nobody asked her to — most of what happened on
> this block yesterday.
>
> Everybody in the Mission reads the neighborhood blog. Nobody knows who
> writes it. #RealWorldGame {{WATCH}}

### C2 — Jules Park

> Jules moved in three weeks ago and is still learning the regulars at
> the café counter. Mornings they run the park's north path. Evenings
> they sketch the block in a notebook they don't let anyone open.
>
> You'll like Jules. They're new here too. {{WATCH}}

### C3 — Dani Reyes

> Dani draws the café's chalkboards — the cup-sleeve doodles regulars
> collect are hers. Nights she draws. Lately, nights she's also somewhere
> else she doesn't talk about.
>
> The block notices more than you'd think. {{WATCH}}

### C4 — Priya Raman

> Three 12s a week on a med-surg floor at SF General. Then the same café
> seat, the same oat latte, the same nod to Marisol. Priya's decompressing.
>
> Everybody on this block has a ritual. Hers is survivable. {{WATCH}}

### C5 — Marcus Bell

> Marcus couriers by bike — he knows every porch on the block and most of
> the gossip that doesn't route through the café. Thursday: drum circle.
> Sunday: he cooks for the whole flat. Rent-interest penance, he calls it.
>
> Ask him what he heard today. {{WATCH}}

### C6 — Carmen Echeverría

> Carmen's retired. She still takes in hemming for cash, takes her
> cafecito on the stoop, and holds the same palm tree in Dolores Park
> every afternoon.
>
> She knows this block's history the way the blog only pretends to. {{WATCH}}

### C7 — Victor Auerbach

> Victor's behind the hardware counter nine to six. He knows every pipe
> in both his buildings by name; Tuesdays he does the repairs himself.
>
> A flyer just said two buildings on Guerrero are quietly for sale.
> Victor hasn't mentioned it. {{WATCH}}

### C8 — Tomás Herrera

> Tomás works the taqueria line five nights a week. Days, he walks the
> supplier loop pricing equipment he can't yet buy — and keeps a paper
> notebook with a business plan and a name in it.
>
> His 3 p.m. coffee at the café is the only fixed point of his afternoon.
> It isn't about the coffee. {{WATCH}}
