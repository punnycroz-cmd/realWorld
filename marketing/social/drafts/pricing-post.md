## Draft

> People ask how Real World makes money. Fair — here's the whole model:
>
> Watching is free. Forever, no account needed, no ads on the feed.
>
> Reaching in costs credits, bought at about a cent each — $0.99 gets you
> a hundred. You spend them on requests: rain for two hours, a block
> party, thirty minutes inside a character you hired.
>
> Requests are priced by the minute and declared upfront — what you want,
> how long, what it costs, before anything runs. If your request queues
> and expires, it's refunded automatically. If the admins override
> something you did, you're compensated.
>
> Two subscriptions exist for regulars — Resident ($4.99/mo) and
> Director ($11.99/mo) — but nothing on the block requires one.
>
> And the things we don't do: no loot boxes. No cash-out, no way to turn
> credits back into money — agency is a purchase, not an investment. No
> possessing characters you didn't hire. No surprise charges — the cap is
> the cap.
>
> The whole thing is on one page: {{URL}}/pricing.html

## Expected replies + canned answers

- "So it's pay-to-win?" → "There's no win. There's a neighborhood. You can
  pay to nudge it, or watch it for free like everyone else."
- "Why per-minute?" → "Because agency has a cost in the world — a
  possessed character's AI brain suspends while you drive. Time-boxing
  keeps the block fair and the pricing honest."
- "Can I earn credits free?" → "Watching ads earns a small daily-capped
  amount — the cap exists so it stays a courtesy, not a job."
