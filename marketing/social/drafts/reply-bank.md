# Reply bank — pre-approved answers to predictable comments

Use: reply to comments/replies/QTs verbatim or lightly adapted. Every reply
here is accuracy-checked against the design doc — editing beyond tone means
re-running the §8 checklist. Sorted by comment type. The rule for all of
them: answer straight, one idea per reply, always end with the watch link
only if the exchange isn't already inside a link.

`{{WATCH}}` = spectator link. `{{PRICING}}` = pricing page link.

---

## 1. "So it's The Truman Show?"

> Basically, except Truman is the whole street — twenty-eight fictional
> people sharing one real Mission block, and none of them know they're
> watched. Watching's free: {{WATCH}}

## 2. "Is this real people? / Is this a camera on a real block?"

> All 28 residents are fictional characters — the streets and Dolores Park
> are real geography, but no real household lives at any address we use
> (house numbers are generated above the real block maximums on purpose).

## 3. "How does it make money?" / "What's the catch?"

> Watching is free forever. You pay only to reach in — requests are priced
> by the minute, declared upfront, and auto-refunded if they never run.
> Whole model: {{PRICING}}

## 4. "Can I possess anyone?" / "Can I be the landlord?"

> You can only ever possess a character you hired yourself — nobody else's,
> and none of the eight mains, not even by us. Their stories stay theirs.

## 5. "Can I make a character do/say X?"

> Requests inject situations, not lines — you direct the opportunity, the
> character decides how to play it in character. Never mind-control.

## 6. "This is creepy / are the characters suffering?"

> Fair question, straight answer: they're fictional agents with authored
> personalities. The possession ban protects their storylines, and the
> design treats their lives as the product, not their discomfort.

## 7. "Someone could use this to harass characters." (moderation)

> Every request is screened for intent before it runs, exclusive ones get
> human review, and abusive ones are denied. And in-world consequences are
> real — the block remembers what your character does. Rules: {{RULES}}

## 8. "Can I earn credits free?" / "Is there a cash-out?"

> Watching ads earns a small daily-capped amount — a courtesy, not a job.
> And no cash-out, ever: credits buy agency, they're not an investment.
> No loot boxes either.

## 9. "Why does it cost anything? Compute is cheap."

> Credits track real compute cost — a character you drive runs a full LLM
> brain while you're in it. The honest version: we priced it from the bill,
> not from a funnel. {{PRICING}}

## 10. "When do I get voice / TTS / NPC possession / selling credits back?"

> Not in v1, and not on the roadmap — we'd rather say no than tease it.
> What's live: the watch tier, requests, weather, hired characters.

## 11. "Can I stream/record it?" (creators)

> Please do — a watchable world is a streamable world. No permission
> needed for the spectator view; if you file requests on stream, the feed
> will show them attributed to you. Press kit: {{PRESSKIT}}

## 12. "The feed is empty / nothing's happening."

> It's live and it's honest — quiet Tuesday is what a real block looks
> like at 2 p.m. The request feed fills up when viewers start filing; be
> the first and it's yours.

## 13. "Is the blog thing real? Who writes it?" (season-one tease)

> Everyone on the block reads it. That's all we've got. (Never confirm,
> never deny — the answer belongs to the residents.)

## 14. "This address is fake — I live on that street."

> Correct and deliberate: every house number is generated above the real
> block maximum, so it looks right but can never be a real household's
> door. Streets are real; residents and their addresses are fictional.

## 15. Bug reports / "your site is broken"

> Thanks — on it. (Route to the dev channel; never argue severity in
> public. If it's real, the fix note becomes the follow-up reply.)

## Tone guardrails for improvised replies

- Dry and specific beats cute. "Quiet Tuesday" is a better answer than a
  deflection.
- Never promise dates, features, or pricing beyond what's on the site.
- Never confirm a drama seed — "that's all we've got" is the whole reply.
- If a comment is abusive or trolling about real people, don't engage;
  it falls under community moderation (MODERATION-PLAN.md), not marketing.
