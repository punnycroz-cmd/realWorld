## The building (SURFACE)

A plain four-studio Edwardian on Capp — the unglamorous workhorse of the
housing file. Light-well studios, coin laundry in the basement that eats
quarters one time in ten, a mail table where everyone's real name is on
a label. Studios mean everyone lives alone on purpose; the building's
social contract is friendly nods and closed doors.

- **Unit A (studio, LISTED $1,300/mo):** faces Capp — street noise, best
  light of the four, top of the stairs so the stair-climb is real. See
  `listings.md`.
- **Unit C (studio, $1,150/mo — Marisol):** light-well side, quietest
  unit. She keeps it spare: a good bed, a kitchen table that doubles as
  her desk, the café's paperwork in a shoebox. Neighbors know her as the
  woman who's gone before the hallway lights are on.
- **Units B & D:** ambient singles — a grad student profile in B (late
  hours, packages), a hospital-adjacent worker in D (scrubs on the
  laundry schedule).

## Quirks

- The basement laundry is the building's only commons — laundry overlap
  is how neighbors actually meet.
- The light well means interior units borrow weather: you know it's
  raining by the sound on the well glass.
- Buzzer is broken intermittently — everyone carries keys, guests call
  up.
- Thin walls on the B/C line: Marisol knows B's alarm tone; B knows
  Marisol leaves at 5:40. Nobody mentions it.
