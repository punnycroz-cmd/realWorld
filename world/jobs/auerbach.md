# Auerbach Hardware — workplace card

**Kind:** hardware store, Mission St · **Interior:** yes (anchor venue)
**Owner:** C7 Victor Auerbach, third generation. He lives upstairs —
9102 Mission St, Unit 2.

## The place

Narrow storefront, deep stock: plumbing aisle, electrical aisle, a key
wall that has cut Mission keys since Victor's grandfather's day. A
counter with a stool, a ledger Victor still keeps alongside the register,
and the Tuesday-repair table — the pile of neighbors' broken things
(VC bindings, lamp sockets, a heater grate that matters to people in
9457-3) that Victor works on Tuesdays because that's when he feels like
it.

## Staff

Victor runs it alone, mostly. **Open role:** counter clerk, $21/h, 30 h/wk
— the hire exists because Victor's knee doesn't do the ladder anymore and
he needs someone for the high shelves and the Tuesday pile. He resents
the need and will never say so; he interviews by handing you a mislabeled
bin of screws and watching what you do.

## Shift shape

- **Weekday mornings:** contractors and Gus's garage — trade accounts,
  fast counter talk, credit lines carried in the ledger.
- **Mid-day:** the DIY stream — Mission renters with a leaking trap, a
  flickering fixture, a key for a new roommate. Victor's teaching counter:
  he tells you how to fix it, sells you the parts, and the advice is free
  because he can't help himself.
- **Saturdays:** the busiest day — apartment projects, landlord supplies
  (he sells to his own tenants' landlords without comment, Mission
  smallness).
- **Tuesday:** the repair table. Neighbors drop things all week; Tuesday
  they get worked on. Cash-in-a-card payments, some bartered, some free —
  the ledger marks most of them "OK," which means he didn't charge.

## Pay & conditions

$21/h, 30 h/wk, biweekly from the register ledger — Victor pays on the
dot and expects punctuality back. The job includes the ladder, the stock
room, and absorbing Victor's silences. Possessed-play bonus: correctly
talking a customer out of a wrong part (the house values the advice over
the sale) is the skill Victor actually pays attention to — +10%.

## Hiring bar

Victor screens for: knowing what a trap is (or admitting you don't —
lying about it is disqualifying), ladder-safe, and the patience to
listen to a repair story twice. He does not want salesmanship; he wants
the counter to still be a place neighbors trust.

## Playing this workplace

- The store is a listening post: everybody's apartment is always
  breaking, so everybody's life crosses this counter eventually. Victor
  hears the neighborhood's maintenance-level truth — whose heat is out,
  whose sink, whose lock got changed suddenly.
- Texture: the key wall is a quiet ledger of who lives where (keys cut
  for moves, breakups, new roommates); Victor treats that knowledge as
  vault material. It is flavor, never a leak channel for cast SECRETS.
- A player-hired clerk's first weeks: screw-bin literacy, learning that
  "OK" in the ledger means free, and earning the right to touch the key
  wall unsupervised — that takes months.
