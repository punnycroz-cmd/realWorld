# Shops & trades — combined card

Folsom Auto & Sons, Needlepointe Tattoo, Bloom & Doom Flowers, Malik's
Mini Mart, Frutería Las Palmas. Mostly cast-staffed; the open roles are
thin — these files exist so the workplaces are playable, not just named.

---

## Folsom Auto & Sons — garage

**Cast job:** A10 Gus, mechanic, $34/h, 40 h.
**Open role:** none currently — a lube/tire slot opens when Gus's nephew
quits, which he does about yearly.

- **The place:** three bays, pit, tire rack, a waiting bench with a
  2009 calendar nobody changed because the photo is a '68 Mustang. Real
  work, honest pricing — the garage's reputation is why Gus has the job.
- **Shift shape:** 8:00–17:00 weekday trade; Saturday mornings are
  walk-ins and regret. Lunch crew hits El Farolote at 11:45 sharp.
- **Culture:** competence hierarchy — Gus diagnoses, the owner signs.
  Jokes are mostly about customers' neglected maintenance; never cruel
  to their faces.
- **Texture:** the parts run to Auerbach for odd fasteners is a daily
  street beat; a mechanic carrying a carburetor down Mission is normal.

## Needlepointe Tattoo — Clarion-adjacent shop

**Cast job:** A14 Bex, artist, ~$30/h commission-weighted (~$4,500/mo).
**Open role:** none — but a front-desk/booking slot exists when Bex's
books out past six weeks.

- **The place:** appointment-shop, no walk-ins after 18:00, flash walls,
  sterilizer hum. Bex's station is the window one — she's the draw.
- **Culture:** appointment-shop quiet; the sound is conversation and
  the machine. Consults are half the job — talking someone out of a
  bad idea kindly is a practiced skill.
- **Texture:** clients' stories land here raw — tattoo chairs get told
  things. House rule: what gets said in the chair stays in the shop.
  Flavor, not a leak channel.

## Bloom & Doom Flowers

**Cast job:** A18 Ida, florist, $25/h, 40 h.
**Open role:** Saturday counter help, $18/h, 8 h — intermittent.

- **The place:** bucket wall, cold case, workbench where weddings and
  funerals are built by the same hands on different days — the name is
  the business model.
- **Shift shape:** market mornings (wholesale run before 7:00), wedding
  weekends front-loaded to Friday builds, funeral work arrives with
  24-hour notice and gets priority.
- **Texture:** Ida reads the neighborhood's life events in orders — the
  condolence bouquet addresses, the anniversary standing orders. She is
  professionally discreet; the card stays surface.

## Malik's Mini Mart — corner store

**Cast job:** A03 Malik, owner-clerk, $18/h equivalent on paper — he owns
the store, so his wage is what's left, which is why he works 40+.
**Open role:** none. (Family shop; his nephew stocks weekends.)

- **The place:** corner-store density — one aisle deep, everything a
  household forgot at 21:00. Lottery tickets, phone top-ups, the good
  hot sauce, milk that's always in date because Malik rotates it himself.
- **Shift shape:** 7:00–23:00 with family coverage; the real rush is
  17:00–20:00, after-work necessities.
- **Culture:** Malik extends informal tab credit to regulars — a
  notebook, not an app; balances settle monthly, mostly. The notebook is
  the store's quiet social contract.
- **Texture:** the store sees the block at its least guarded — midnight
  runs, emergency diapers, the exact shape of everyone's bad week.

## Frutería Las Palmas — fruit stand, Dolores at 19th

**Cast job:** A07 Luz, vendor, ~$4,000/mo cash, 55 h informal.
**Open role:** none — it's her stand; a second chair exists when she
visits family.

- **The place:** sidewalk stand — mangoes, cut fruit with chile, aguas.
  Informal economy texture: cash, regulars, her own prices.
- **Shift shape:** park hours, weather-dependent — foggy mornings halve
  it, festival weekends triple it. Rain closes the stand entirely.
- **Texture:** the stand is a social anchor — Luz knows the park crowd
  by face and order. She holds people's change, watches people's bags,
  feeds the guy who sleeps near the tennis courts. Surface texture only.
