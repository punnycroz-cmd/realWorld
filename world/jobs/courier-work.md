# Courier & gig work — combined card

Two ways to ride for money: the **Flying Pannier Courier Co-op** (C5
Marcus's employer) and **MuleIt** app delivery (A06 Kofe's). The contrast
between them is deliberate world texture — solidarity vs. churn.

---

## Flying Pannier Courier Co-op

**Cast job:** C5 Marcus, ~$18/h equivalent, ~30 h variable + tips.
**Open to hires:** yes — the co-op takes new riders twice a year when a
member ages out or moves up; flag the slot as "intermittent" on the board.

- **The org:** worker-owned, fourteen members, no boss — a Monday-night
  meeting that runs long and a locker wall on Treat Ave that is the
  "office." Dispatch is a shared radio channel + a whiteboard. Rival
  energy with the apps is real: Pannier riders know MuleIt pays less and
  say so, loudly, at the Mudhaus counter.
- **How pay works:** per-job tags, not per-hour — the $18/h equivalent is
  what a decent week averages. Rain pays more (surge the members vote on
  themselves). Slow weeks are real: February can dip under $14/h
  equivalent, which is why Marcus's rent share runs late.
- **The work:** restaurant runs (El Farolote's catering, Il Delfino's
  overflow), legal-filing runs downtown, the flower/parts/miscellaneous
  layer. Fixed routes don't exist; the radio allocates by who's nearest.
- **Culture:** knowing the city is the skill — which building's freight
  elevator is broken, which doorman signs, which alley saves four minutes.
  Members carry each other's slow weeks informally; debts inside the co-op
  are remembered forever and never written down.
- **Hiring bar:** own bike, one member vouches, you ride a loaded trial
  week. They screen for judgment calls, not speed — a rider who flags a
  bad address beats a fast rider who doesn't.
- **Texture:** rain transforms the job (danger + surge pay + everyone
  orders in); the Monday meeting is where co-op democracy produces both
  fairness and forty-minute arguments about nothing.

## MuleIt (app delivery)

**Cast job:** A06 Kofe. **Open:** always hiring — that's the problem.

- **The org:** a phone. No office, no coworkers, no meeting — Kofe's
  colleagues are the other riders he recognizes at pickup counters and
  never speaks to. The nod at Mudhaus is the entire culture.
- **How pay works:** algorithmic offers, ~$17/h equivalent on a good
  week, tips make or break it. No surge you can vote on — the app surges
  and you take it or don't. Rating 4.8+ keeps the good offers flowing;
  one bad week of ratings and the offers thin out (this is Kofe's quiet
  anxiety).
- **The work:** food delivery loops — El Farolote, Il Delfino, the chains
  on Mission. Wait time at restaurants is unpaid and everyone feels it.
- **Hiring bar:** a phone and a bike. There is no interview; there is a
  background check and a gear deposit.
- **Texture:** the app is the weather — offers arrive, heat maps shift,
  nothing to negotiate with. Kofe stacking MuleIt hours against his real
  life is ambient texture, not a storyline to surface.
