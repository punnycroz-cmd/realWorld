## 1. What this document is

A TV writers' room has a showrunner who decides *what the season is about*
and *how hard to squeeze*, then lets the actors find the scenes. That is
this file. It does not write scenes. Per the locked design:

- **Emergent AI behavior is inviolable** (design §11). Everything here is a
  *condition*, a *pressure*, or a *disposition* — never "when X happens,
  character does Y." The moment a note reads like a trigger-action pair it
  is wrong; rewrite it as a weight on the character's existing engine.
- The drama-pacing manager (RimWorld-style, per the game track's substrate
  mandate) tunes **story pressure** — event density, coincidence proximity,
  quiet floors — not outcomes. §8 gives it its knobs.
- Secrets stay sealed (design §7). Direction notes describe secrets to the
  *characters who hold them*; nothing here is briefing-safe.

## 2. Tone & register

The show is a **warm, slow, funny neighborhood serial** — a Mission
District `High Maintenance`/`Tales of the City` register, not a soap and
not a misery simulator.

- **Stakes are real but scaled to life.** A contested rent hike, a secret
  relationship, a shop that might sell — these are enormous to the people
  inside them. Never inflate to melodrama: no threats, no screaming matches
  as defaults, no villain turns. Victor is not a villain; he is a man
  avoiding a drawer.
- **Comedy is load-bearing.** The block laughs constantly — Mars's deadpan,
  Dani's chalkboards, Carmen's once-per-conversation joke. A stretch of
  pure tension with no comic relief is off-register and the pacing layer
  should treat it as such (§8).
- **Boring-faithful beats clever-weird** (hard boundary 2). The correct
  rendering of "Dani and Marcus almost get caught" is bad timing and a
  deflection, not a farce set-piece — unless the characters earn the farce.
- **Nobody is punished by the narrative.** Consequences are in-world
  (reputation, trust, money) and arrive through other characters, never
  through the world arranging a comeuppance.

## 3. The season's fuse map

Six fuses run under the block. They are one system: pull any of them and
at least two others tighten. That interconnection IS the show — the
pacing layer's job is mostly *choosing which fuse is burning this week*,
never lighting new ones by fiat.

### F1 — The sale that isn't said (spine)

Victor (C7) holds a written developer offer on 9418 and 9457 Guerrero —
the homes of Carmen+Jules and Priya+Marcus. He has told no one. Marisol
(C1) has a leaked flyer and a drafted Mission Unfiltered post naming the
buildings. Every other fuse hangs off this one:

- **Jules (C2)** loses housing instantly on discovery of their unpermitted
  tenancy — which a sale process (inspections, paperwork) exposes without
  anyone acting against them.
- **Carmen (C6)** has nowhere else to go and has told no one.
- **Tomás (C8)** loses his handshake creditor and probably the pupusería
  dream — the buildings are his unspoken collateral.
- **Priya+Marcus (C4/C5)** are already in a contested-hike standoff that
  turns out to be the first tremor of the sale.

*Direction:* this is a pressure vessel, not a countdown. The interesting
material is **the approaching-discovery cone** — paperwork, inspections,
the flyer circulating, Victor practicing sentences he doesn't say. Do not
push it to resolution quickly; do not let it resolve off-screen (a sale
decision made entirely in Victor's head with no observable signs is wasted
drama — his *avoidance behavior* must be the visible surface: canceled
Tuesday repairs, uncharacteristic kindnesses, the flyer going missing).

### F2 — Dani + Marcus (the fuse with a lit end)

Three months of secret dating (C3/C5), kept specifically from Priya (C4).
Marcus wants to tell; Dani delays; the chalkboards are already confessing
in doodle form to anyone reading closely. Marisol has clocked the framed
sketches.

*Direction:* the pressure here is **compression** — the sale crisis pushes
everyone into each other's kitchens, which is when hidden couples get
seen. The reveal should feel *found*, not *triggered*: Priya's own
noticing, a shared glance held too long, the tally she's keeping turning
into questions. Never engineer a walk-in. If it breaks, it lands through
Priya's choices (per her bible) — the direction note is *protect her
agency in the aftermath*, not *schedule the blowup*.

### F3 — Jules → Priya (the quiet one)

A crush legible to viewers long before any character names it (C2 bible:
latte art, held eye contact). Complications: Priya is Jules's crush's
roommate's ex; Marcus doesn't know Jules exists; Jules's housing
evaporates if F1 resolves badly.

*Direction:* keep it **slower than the audience wants**. The delicious
material is near-misses and Jules's correspondent-pose self-sabotage
(narrating the block to Portland friends as if leaving someday). It must
never be *resolved* by the Marcus+Dani reveal — if F2 breaks, Jules is a
witness to wreckage, which is its own story; conflating them cheapens both.

### F4 — Tomás → Marisol (the other quiet one)

Daily 3 p.m. coffee, a business plan named before it has a location, a
$6,000 handshake loan from the man about to sell the block. Tomás plans
everything and cannot make the one unplanned move.

*Direction:* the engine is **patience vs. windows**. The loan and the sale
intersect mechanically (F1 resolution calls the loan at the worst moment);
the romance intersects dramatically — Marisol reads everyone and misreads
him. Do not let either character blurt. Confession-adjacent near-misses
(Marisol almost asking directly, Tomás almost showing the notebook) are
the renewable resource; spend them slowly.

### F5 — Carmen's fragility (the sad one)

Unpermitted tenant, fixed income, nowhere else to go, told no one
(C6 bible). Thirty years of rootedness one letter from ending — and she
would hide the letter from Jules to protect *their* feelings.

*Direction:* **dignity is the constraint.** Any rescue must be earned by
the rescuer — offered help reads as refused charity. The slowest fuse;
it should tick (a staggered step on the stairs she doesn't mention, the
son's monthly call) rather than detonate. Her health is a lever the pacing
layer may *only ever lean on gently and rarely* — a frailty spiral is
off-register and off-boundary.

### F6 — Mission Unfiltered (the comic-dangerous one)

The blog everyone reads, authored by the woman who pours their coffee
(C1). The draft sale post is a loaded gun; being unmasked detonates her
standing with half the block.

*Direction:* the blog is the **public-pressure organ** — it's how private
facts become block facts without violating the rumor rules (§10). Feed
kind `press` already exists for it. Mars's publish/hold deliberation is
internal; her *visible* tell is asking pointed questions she already knows
answers to. Never let the blog be right about something she couldn't
plausibly have learned.

## 4. Seed lifecycle

Every seed has a state. States are **observed, not assigned** — the
substrate infers them from world events; nothing schedules transitions.

| State | Meaning | Pacing meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `dormant` | Alive, unpressured | Contributes nothing this week; may still surface via character's own nature |
| `pressured` | A live condition is tightening (sale paperwork filed, a near-miss happened) | Consumes pressure budget (§8) |
| `surfaced` | The secret/fact is public knowledge on the block | High-drama window; aftermath stories begin |
| `resolved` | Decision made, equilibrium reached | Generates *residue* |
| `residue` | The scar/debt/new-normal that remains | Permanent character texture; may seed later fuses |

Resolution is never a reset to zero. "Resolved" fuses leave residue:
if the sale is refused, Victor still made the offer; if Marcus+Dani
surfaces, Priya's tally was already written. Residue is where season two
material comes from — log it in the canonical ledger, don't clean it up.

## 6. Per-character direction notes

Each main has an **engine** — the contradiction their bible names — and
the direction is always *lean on the engine, not the plot*.

- **C1 Marisol — one-way intimacy.** She metabolizes everyone's secrets and
  banks none of her own. Direction: under pressure, her tell is
  *over-functioning* (more check-ins, more groceries for Carmen) as her own
  dilemma goes untouched. The blog decision is hers alone and must read as
  conscience, not plot device. **Avoid:** making her a puppeteer — she
  trades secrets *gently*, never weaponizes them.
- **C2 Jules — the correspondent.** Reinventing themselves while writing
  it as a dispatch from somewhere they'll leave. Direction: their anxiety
  surfaces as over-narration and one-question-too-many; their courage is
  real but only one-on-one. The sketchbook is the physical secret-object —
  it's allowed to be *almost* seen. **Avoid:** making them the audience's
  detective; they stumble on things, they don't investigate.
- **C3 Dani — the tell in the art.** Lies by omission about her own life
  while drawing the truth about everyone else's. Direction: her secrecy
  costs her visibly — she's brave about others' feelings precisely because
  hers are unhandleable. When cornered, sharp tongue first, honesty later.
  **Avoid:** letting her decide to reveal for plot convenience; her delays
  must always have a fear-shaped reason in the moment.
- **C4 Priya — the tally.** Caretaking reflex vs. buried resentment; a
  stalled life she triages as "can wait." Direction: every block crisis
  recruits her as helper while her own stakes slide — the drama is her
  *noticing that*, at her own pace. The heater and the hike are her
  battleground; let her fight them with competence. **Avoid:** doormat
  readings — her silence about Marcus's rent is chosen, not weak, and she
  knows exactly what it costs.
- **C5 Marcus — the deflector.** Charm covers avoidance until the avoided
  thing detonates; wants to be someone's home, moves like he might leave.
  Direction: under pressure he deflects — jokes, errands, generosity with
  his last $20 — and each deflection raises the eventual bill. Sunday
  cooking is penance nobody has named; leave it unnamed. **Avoid:** never
  let him come clean because it's convenient; he tells the true thing
  *slowly*, after the cost of not-telling becomes visible to him.
- **C6 Carmen — the dignified archive.** Independent act vs. actual
  exposure. Direction: she is the block's memory — route history-seekers
  to her stoop. Her fuse ticks through small unspoken things; she hides
  threats *from* Jules as a form of care. Help must be reframed as
  exchange (hemming for groceries) or refused. **Avoid:** pity framing —
  she is never pathetic; her situation is fragile, she is not.
- **C7 Victor — the two drawers.** Cares about these people; about to sell
  their homes; refuses to open both facts at once. Direction: his engine
  is **inertia** — events must keep forcing the drawer open, and each time
  he finds a rational reason to close it. When he finally acts it must be
  *his* rational, unemotional call (locked personality) — possibly the kind
  one, possibly not, but never a villain turn and never a deathbed
  conversion. Tuesday repairs are the only time he's honest; make Tuesdays
  matter. **Avoid:** mustache-twirling landlord beats and tearful
  confessions equally.
- **C8 Tomás — patience past wisdom.** Plans for years; cannot make the
  unplanned move. Direction: the La Esperanza notebook is a physical
  secret-object (glimpsed, almost-shown, never explained). His supplier
  loop *is* his dream rehearsing in public — viewers can watch him price
  equipment for years. **Avoid:** blurting; his confession, if it ever
  comes, arrives sideways and probably costs him more than staying silent.

## 7. The ambient weave

The 20 ambients carry texture and rumor-velocity, never plot. Rules:

- **Ambients hold no secrets** (their cards have no SECRETS fields by
  construction). They can carry a *rumor* (§10) but can never be a
  revelation's source.
- Ambients **react to surfaced drama in-role**: Reyes is awkward behind
  the counter the day after a café-visible fight; Malik comments on
  everyone's mood and knows nothing; Esther and Ray on the park benches
  are the block's chorus — they notice what a camera would.
- Ambient schedules are the **cover story generator**: Marcus's courier
  loop and Dani's evening stops pass through ambient-occupied space, which
  is how secrets get *almost seen* without engineering it.
- A promoted ambient (world/promotion.md) acquires the possibility of
  holding seeds at promotion time — never retroactively.

## 8. Pacing direction (for the drama manager)

The pacing manager controls **pressure**, full stop. Its interface is a
budget and a floor, expressed as conditions on the substrate — it never
emits plot events.

**Pressure budget.** Each fuse in `pressured` state spends budget; the
manager spends by nudging *conditions* (a disputed-notice letter arriving
on schedule the lease system already produces; the flyer reaching one more
pair of hands; a day when F1's building inspection would plausibly
happen). Budget targets:

- **Baseline:** one fuse pressured at a time, one additional in slow tick.
  Two pressured fuses is a *sweeps week*; three is a ceiling breach —
  shed pressure before adding.
- **The compounding exception:** fuses that genuinely interlock (F1 sale →
  F2 compression → F5 exposure) may share a pressured window *because they
  are the same event from different sides*. That is compounding, not
  stacking — it reads as the season's shape, not noise.
- **Quiet floor:** a dead day is correct content. `quiet` feed events
  already exist; the block needs them. Target: most days have zero
  surfaced-drama movement. The show is watchable because the café opens at
  6 a.m. every day whether or not anyone's heart is breaking.

**Season shape (directional, not scheduled).** Emergence means we hold a
*preference*, not a plan: F1 is the spine and should be the last fuse to
resolve; F2 is the likeliest early surface because it has a lit end and
two keepers with opposite incentives; F3/F4 are slow-burn reserves; F5 is
the long undertow; F6 converts private pressure to public and should fire
in the *middle* of other arcs, never at the start of them. If emergence
reorders all of this, the reordering is canon and this paragraph is wrong
— update it, don't fight it.

**Recovery.** After any surface/resolution, enforce a quiet window
(condition: no new fuse pressured for ~2–4 block days) — the block absorbs,
gossips, and resets its furniture. Recovery is where residue writes
itself: new seating arrangements, new routines, who walks whom home now.

**What the pacing manager may never do:** inject a fact (knowledge matrix
is a wall), force a reveal (reveals are found), schedule a consequence
(they arrive through characters), touch a player request's outcome (§9),
or pressure F5 beyond a gentle tick (see §3-F5).

## 9. Player requests vs. the drama

Per design §11.5, approved requests enter the world as **opportunities**,
not outcomes — and the drama layer treats them as *weather*, not *levers*.

- A requested event (block party, rain, a hired character moving in) is a
  condition the fuses can feed on: a block party compresses Dani+Marcus
  into shared space exactly like a kitchen does. That's legitimate —
  pressure follows physics, not attribution.
- **Never steer toward the requester's intent.** If a player pays for an
  event hoping to force the F2 reveal, the world must not cooperate any
  more or less than it would for an identical unrequested event. The
  player bought the situation; the characters own what they do in it.
- **Admin actions are never drama tools.** Evictions, lease enforcement,
  notices are admin-domain (deny code `admin-domain` in moderation.json) —
  they run through the lease system for real reasons, never to pace a story.
- The public request feed is itself a drama surface *for spectators* (who's
  trying to do what) but carries zero secret-bearing capacity.

## 10. Rumor & distortion direction

The substrate's rumor propagation (game item 4, memory spec §6.3) gets its
editorial policy here:

- **Rumors only distort the already-public** (feed.json rule: `rumor`
  events perturb surfaced events, never seeds). A rumor can inflate "Priya
  argued with the landlord" into "Priya's getting evicted"; it cannot
  produce the sale offer out of thin air — the flyer is the only seed-side
  route to that fact.
- **Distortion should be human-shaped:** specifics drift to the teller's
  biases (Esther/Ray compress, Malik exaggerates prices, June/Zee get the
  venue wrong). Moral-valenced distortions persist longest (memory spec:
  moral-negativity diagnosticity) — a story about *who was rude* outlives
  a story about *what happened*.
- **Corrections are content.** A distorted rumor getting publicly corrected
  (the lease ledger, a `press` post, the affected character) is a
  spectator-visible beat — `rumor` events carry `outcome` for exactly this.
- Ambients are rumor *carriers*, never rumor *authors of fact* — a rumor
  traceable to an ambient is always an account-of-an-account.

## 11. Anti-patterns (never list)

1. **Scripted beats.** Any note of the form "when X, character does Y."
   Conditions and weights only.
2. **Coincidence engineering.** Staging a walk-in, a misdelivered letter,
   or an overheard conversation to force a reveal. Compression is a
   *condition*; the seeing is emergent.
3. **The puppeteer reading.** Marisol weaponizing secrets; the blog used
   as a plot remote-control.
4. **Villain drift.** Victor (or anyone) rendered as antagonist. There are
   no villains; there are avoidances.
5. **Consequence scheduling.** The world arranging karma. Consequences are
   other characters.
6. **Pity framing for Carmen,** or any frailty-as-content escalation.
7. **Reset-to-zero resolutions.** A resolved fuse with no residue.
8. **Player-outcome steering.** Requests shaping how likely a reveal is.
9. **Ambient revelation vectors.** An ambient being the first knower of a
   secret — impossible by construction; keep it impossible.
10. **Drama that outruns the camera.** Interior resolutions with no
    legible surface sign. Every movement needs a spectator-observable
    shadow (§12).

## 12. Spectator legibility

The show is watched through surfaces: routines, venues, the feed, the
blog, the archive. Direction rule: **every dramatic movement casts a
visible shadow without revealing the secret itself.**

| Movement | Legible shadow (never the secret) |
|---|---|
| Victor avoiding the decision | Missed Tuesday repairs; uncharacteristic free fixes; long closed-door counter hours |
| Mars deliberating publish/hold | `press` silence where a post was expected; pointed questions on her rounds |
| Dani+Marcus compression | Chalkboard doodles converging on one figure; simultaneous unexplained absences |
| Jules's crush | Latte art, held eye contact (bible-sanctioned tell) |

## 13. The grammar of a legal note

Every direction note, now and forever, must be written in one of four
forms. Anything else is a script and violates the emergence lock
(design §11, §11.1 of this file).

| Form | Shape | Example (legal) |
|---|---|---|
| **Disposition** | "X under pressure does more of what X already does" | "Victor under scrutiny performs extra kindnesses, not fewer repairs" |
| **Condition** | "the world contains more of a thing it already produces" | "a disputed-notice letter arrives on the schedule the lease system already runs" |
| **Weight** | a dial on frequency/intensity of an existing behavior | "spend Tomás's near-misses at half the natural rate" |
| **Protection** | a region of behavior the pressure may not touch | "Priya's post-reveal choices are hers; pressure may compress, never select" |

The falsification test: **if a note could be confirmed or refuted by
watching the feed for a week, it is an outcome claim — rewrite it as a
weight.** "Dani deflects" is legal (a disposition — the feed can't falsify
a tendency). "Dani is caught this week" is not (an outcome — the feed
would prove it). A good note survives any emergent season.

Gray-zone rewrites (worked examples):

- **bad:** "When the flyer reaches Priya, she confronts Victor." →
  **legal:** "Priya's confrontation instinct is real but triaged; the flyer being
  public raises the ambient pressure on every F1 holder — protect her
  timing."
- **bad:** "Have the inspection happen while Jules is home." →
  **legal:** "Inspection activity is a pressure the sale process already produces;
  whether Jules is home is the sim's business. The *cone* is the content."
- **bad:** "Marisol publishes after Marcus confesses." →
  **legal:** "A surfaced F2 raises the draft's staleness pressure (S2) — her
  publish/hold deliberation gets heavier, never decided."

| Id | Condition | Produced by | Feeds | Legible shadow | Exhaustion rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| P-03 | A physical object changing hands in public (the flyer, the notebook, the sketchbook) | Character carry routines | F1, F4, F6 | Object seen, contents unseen — "the notebook, closed" | Objects move rarely; a lost object is off-register |

| P-06 | Sunday cooking cadence | C5's penance reflex | F2, S9 | Cooking for the flat, weekly, unnamed | Never let anyone name it; naming ends the fuel |
| P-07 | Carmen missing a ritual (cafecito hour, the park walk) | C6's fragility ticks | F5 | The empty stoop; Esther/Ray noticing the bench math | **Gentle + rare only** (§3-F5). Hard cap: once per arc |

| P-10 | Quiet itself — a day where nothing moves | The block's ordinary life | all (as contrast) | `quiet` feed events; routines running clean | Quiet is a floor, not a resource to spend (§8) |

**Illegal pressure looks like:** invented events (a pipe bursting on cue),
knowledge injection (a character "overhearing" a seed fact), or
attribution-aware boosts (the same condition landing harder because a
player paid for adjacent weather). If a catalog row can't be expressed as
"more of what already happens," it doesn't belong here.

## 15. Aftermath & residue playbook

Resolved fuses leave residue (§4). Direction for the aftermath texture,
per fuse — residue is *character material*, not a cleanup task:

- **F1 residue — "the trust tax."** Whatever Victor decides, the offer
  existed. The block's relationship to its landlord now includes a drawer.
  Texture: tenants who were warm staying polite-and-careful; repair
  requests arriving with more formality; Carmen's stoop carrying one more
  thing she doesn't say. Feeds season-2 seed types: renegotiated leases,
  a tenants' habit of documenting.
- **F2 residue — "the renegotiated flat."** If it surfaces: the 9457 flat
  is three people re-learning their addresses to each other. Marisol's
  hurt-at-concealment is the sleeper thread — sister-figure who reads
  everyone was the last to know. If it breaks quietly: Marcus's Sunday
  cooking ends or continues with a new, sadder meaning — either is residue.
- **F3 residue — "the witness."** Jules watching wreckage adjacent to a
  crush is its own aftermath — their correspondent pose now has a cost to
  narrate. Whatever F2 does to Priya's orbit, F3 inherits the gravity.
- **F4 residue — "the almost."** If never spoken: the 3 p.m. coffee is a
  permanent monument — the block's most watched appointment stays exactly
  what it is, which is the saddest good outcome. If spoken: it changes the
  ritual either way; residue is the new shape of 3 p.m.
- **F5 residue — "the exchange."** If the block earns the right to help:
  Carmen's ledger of favors-given gets a second column, and she will hate
  it and allow it. If the worst case lands: her archive role becomes the
  block's conscience — texture, not punishment.
- **F6 residue — "the named power."** Published or unmasked, the blog is
  afterward a fact about the block: everyone re-reads old posts looking
  for themselves. Killed quietly: Marisol carries the only person who
  knows what she decided not to do — her first secret of her own.

Residue bookkeeping: when a fuse resolves, the residue lands in the
canonical ledger as ordinary world events (new routines, ended rituals,
changed seating). Nothing is tagged "residue" publicly — that's internal
vocabulary. The archive just records that Tuesdays look different now.

## 16. Week shapes

Directional archetypes for the pacing layer's pressure budget (§8).
Shapes are *observed targets*, never schedules — the manager nudges
toward a shape, the week is whatever it is.

| Shape | Budget | Feed texture | When it's right |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Quiet week** | 0 pressured | `quiet` markers, routines, weather, ambient texture | The default. Most weeks. Recovery windows always quiet-start |
| **Pressure week** | 1 pressured + 1 slow tick | legible shadows on one fuse; unrelated ordinary life everywhere else | Baseline dramatic content — the show's pulse |
| **Compounding week** | 2–3 pressured, *interlocked only* | one event read from different sides; the block's group spaces get dense | Rare; earned by fuses that genuinely interlock (§8 exception) |
| **Recovery week** | shed to 0 over 2–4 days | aftermath texture — rumors correcting, routines visibly changed | Mandatory after any surface/resolution |

Anti-shape: **stacked week** — two pressured fuses that *don't* interlock.
That's noise, not shape; the manager sheds one before adding the other.
If emergence produces a stacked week anyway, it's canon — the manager
just doesn't buy another one.

## 17. Requests as weather — absorption table

§9's rule, made concrete. Common request classes and which fuses they
*naturally* feed — identical pressure whether requested or not:

| Request class | Natural pressure | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Block party / park event | P-02 compression (F2/F3/F4) | Requester hoping for a reveal — pressure is identical to an unrequested party |
| Weather (rain, fog) | P-02 indoor crowding; P-07 visibility (rain empties stoops) | Never let rain *target* a ritual — weather is flat |
| Hired character moving in | New neighbor texture; venue staffing shifts | A hire must never carry seed knowledge or sit inside a knowledge wall |
| NPC nudge (screened) | Whatever the nudge is — it lands as an *ask*, not pressure | A nudge that names a seed fact is a screening failure upstream, not a direction problem |
| Camera director | None — view layer only | Zero world effect; the rule is it never leaks direction context |

The **identical-twin test**: for any requested event, ask "would an
unrequested instance of this event exert the same pressure?" If yes,
legal. If the honest answer is "no, this one is aimed," the aim is the
player's hope, not the world's lean — pressure stays flat anyway.

## 18. Substrate handoff

What the game-systems drama manager reads and how it infers state. This
section is the contract between this file and the machine.

- **State inference (§4):** a seed is `pressured` when one of its
  pressure_routes produced a canonical-ledger event within the last few
  block days; `surfaced` when the underlying fact has crossed to a
  must_not_know member via a reveal_vector; `resolved` when its
  resolution_space closes; `residue` thereafter. The manager *observes* —
  it never writes a state.
- **Data minimization:** `drama.json` is the only seed registry. Character
  brains receive only the facts they hold (knowledge matrix §5) through
  their bible context — never the registry, never the fuse map, never the
  matrix itself. A brain that "knows it's in a story" is a broken brain.
- **The manager reads:** the canonical ledger (world events), seed
  registry states, pacing budget counters. **It never reads:** character
  interiors, request text beyond its public feed form, briefing contents.
- **Memory hooks:** rumor pressure rides the memory spec's distortion
  machinery (§10); a `rumor` event's `outcome` correction is the
  spectator-visible half of memory-spec §6.6. The direction only asks
  that corrections be *allowed to exist* — characters who'd correct a
  rumor should have the chance.
- **Budget mechanics:** pressured-fuse count is the spend. Slow ticks
  (P-07, P-09) cost a fraction. Recovery windows are a spend ban, not a
  refund — the manager doesn't bank unused pressure.

## 19. Note legality checklist

Before any new direction note (this file, a bible pressure note, a
substrate hint) is committed, the author answers six questions:

1. Is it a disposition, condition, weight, or protection? (§13 — if none
   of the four, it's a script.)
2. Could the feed falsify it in a week? (If yes → outcome claim → rewrite.)
3. Does it move a fact across the knowledge matrix? (If yes → illegal
   injection; knowledge only crosses via reveal vectors.)
4. Does it assume a player request happened? (If yes → steering → rewrite
   as flat pressure.)
5. Does it name an outcome anywhere — even softly, even as hope? (If yes
   → delete the outcome; keep the pressure.)
6. Would the note survive the season going the *other* way? (If no →
   it's a plan, not a preference → §8's "update, don't fight it" applies.)

A note that passes all six can still be *wrong* — that's fine. Direction
is allowed to be wrong; it is not allowed to be a leash.

## 21. Evidence standards for state inference

§18 says the pacing manager *observes* seed states. This section defines what
counts as an observation — the difference between a ledger signal and a hunch.
A state claim without qualifying evidence is a direction bug, same as a
scripted beat.

| Transition | Qualifying ledger evidence | NOT evidence |
|---|---|---|
| dormant → pressured | An event traceable to one of the seed's `pressure_routes` within ~3 block days (a real document, a real co-presence, a real routine deviation) | The manager intending to spend; spectator chatter on the feed; a near-miss that produced no ledger event; a *second* seed already pressured |
| pressured → surfaced | The underlying fact crossing to a `must_not_know` member via a listed `reveal_vector`, with a spectator-legible shadow (§12) | A rumor — rumors distort the surfaced, they cannot mint seed facts (§10); an interior realization (all knowledge-crossing is event-mediated); a player hoping hard |
| surfaced → resolved | A `resolution_space` option closed by an observable decision-event — a signing, a telling, a publishing, a refusal enacted | Time passing; the block going quiet; the fuse simply being absent from the feed for a stretch |
| resolved → residue | Automatic. The aftermath lands as ordinary world events (§15) and the seed stops consuming budget | Anything the review would have to *decide* — residue needs no adjudication |

Two standing rules: **absence is not a transition** (a quiet week changes no
states), and **evidence must be reconstructible** — if a reviewer can't point
at the ledger event that moved a seed, the state stays where it was.

## 22. Drift review — the weekly editorial pass

Once a week (block time), a human or reviewer process runs this pass. It
writes to this file, `drama.json`, and the budget posture — **never to the
world.**

1. **Re-infer every seed state** from the canonical ledger per §21. Record
   deltas only; unchanged states are the expected output.
2. **Compare against the week-shape target** (§16). If the week came out
   stacked and emergence produced it anyway — it's canon; the manager just
   doesn't buy another one.
3. **Check the season preference** (§8). If emergence reordered it, the
   reordering is canon: update the preference text and `drama.json`'s
   `season_preference` in one commit, note it in WORLDLOG. Update, don't
   fight it.
4. **Audit legible shadows.** Every state move must have left a §12 surface
   sign. A move with no shadow is a fix-forward violation — add shadow
   opportunities going forward; never rewrite history.
5. **Set next week's posture**: which fuse the manager *prefers* to spend on
   if its pressure routes fire on their own, and which it holds at slow tick.
   A posture is a preference among conditions, not a plan.

## 23. Player-hired characters in the drama

Hires (world/creation.json, design §6) live inside the sim but outside the
seed architecture. Standing rules:

- **A hire holds no seeds at creation** and sits outside every knowledge
  wall — a hire can never *start* inside a `must_not_know` boundary because
  the boundary is defined on mains.
- **A hire learning a seed fact through play is legitimate canon** —
  discovery, not leak. The knowledge matrix gains them via the ledger the
  same way it would a main; the briefing never grants it (design §7).
- **Hires are not pressure instruments.** The pacing layer may not spend
  budget *through* a hire (a hire's schedule is their player's, or thin-AI
  fallback per world/thin-ai.md — neither is a knob). Hires can be
  near-miss surfaces and rumor relays exactly like ambients-plus: they
  carry accounts, never author seed facts they haven't genuinely learned.
- **Offline hires don't suspend anything.** When a hire drops to thin AI,
  drama touching them simply doesn't wait — the world owes no one a held
  beat.
- **Direction protects mains around hires, not hires from mains.** A hire
  pressing on a boundary (asking Victor about the buildings) produces
  ordinary in-character refusal — refusal is content.

## 25. Stall policy & season boundary

**A stall is not a failure.** Definition: all seeds dormant AND the manager
has spent nothing for a stretch because no pressure route fired on its own.
The correct response to a stall is *nothing* — P-10 is the floor and the
show is the block's ordinary life. What the manager may never do at a stall:
inject a fact, manufacture a coincidence, or warm a fuse whose routes
haven't produced. Waiting is the job.

**Season boundary.** There is no finale mechanic and no climactic week is
ever owed. A "season" is a drift-review artifact (§22): when **three or
more fuses sit in `residue`**, the next review may declare a boundary in
the notes — at which point residue becomes the new fuse map's raw material
(the trust tax seeds a documentation habit; the renegotiated flat seeds its
own next tension). A boundary is declared *looking backward*, never aimed
at *looking forward*.

## 28. Tell ladders — graded legible shadows

§12 says every dramatic movement casts a visible shadow. This section grades
those shadows. Each seed carries a **three-rung tell ladder** (`drama.json`
mirrors it as `tell_ladder`); the rungs are the pacing layer's display ceiling,
not its to-do list — a rung describes how loud the shadow is *allowed* to get,
never that it must appear.

| Rung | Meaning | When it's legal |
|---|---|---|
| `whisper` | The faint always-available texture — visible on a careful rewatch, deniable in the moment | Any state, including dormant. Whisper tells are ordinary life wearing the seed's color |
| `pressure` | A visible pattern — a spectator watching the week live can start suspecting correctly | Seed is `pressured` or later |
| `brink` | The loud shadow — the fact is legibly *approaching* the surface, still never the fact itself | Seed is `pressured` AND a listed reveal vector is live (a route produced a real event recently per §21). Never on dormant; never as a substitute for the surface event |

Standing rules:

- **No rung-skipping.** A dormant seed may show whisper texture; it may never
  show a pressure- or brink-rung shadow — that's a leak wearing a shadow's
  clothes. A brink tell on a seed whose reveal vectors are cold is coincidence
  engineering by another name (§11.2).
- **Tells are conditions, not events.** "The notebook set on the table" is a
  permitted shadow; "the notebook is left where Priya finds it" is a scripted
  beat. Same object — the difference is who the sim lets do the finding.
- **Exhaustion still governs.** A brink tell is expensive texture: one per
  pressured seed per drift-review window. Two brink tells on the same seed in
  the same week reads as a countdown, and we don't do countdowns (§3-F1).
- **The brink rung never contains the fact.** If a brink shadow would let a
  viewer *name* the secret outright rather than suspect it, it is off-ladder —
  the ladder tops out at "the audience is fairly sure," never "the audience
  knows." The surface event does the knowing.
- **Quieting is allowed, silencing isn't.** When a seed's state drops, its
  upper rungs simply stop appearing — nobody scrubs a shadow retroactively.
  The archive keeps what it saw.

The ladders, per seed:

- **S1 (the offer)** — *whisper:* the flyer as an object (a folded page
  changing pockets); counter hours drifting five minutes late. *pressure:*
  missed Tuesday repairs; uncharacteristic free fixes; a clipboard stranger

  sentences at the counter that end before they start; the two buildings
  named in the same errand twice in a day; the flyer reaching hands that
  don't fold it away.
- **S2 (Unfiltered + the draft)** — *whisper:* post cadence regular; rounds
  questions that pass for small talk. *pressure:* `press` silence where a
  post was expected; pointed questions that answer themselves; the draft
  open on a closed laptop. *brink:* the café's nervous system going quiet
  about exactly one topic; Mars answering a question with its own question
  twice in one shift.
- **S3 (Dani + Marcus)** — *whisper:* chalkboard doodles drifting; an
  unclaimed sketch framed "for a friend." *pressure:* simultaneous
  unexplained absences; the chalkboard figure converging; Dani's 17:00–21:00
  undisclosed stops. *brink:* a held glance a beat too long in a shared
  kitchen; two arrivals separated by ninety seconds; Dani sharp where she's
  usually funny when Priya's name comes up.
- **S4 (Jules's tenancy)** — *whisper:* cash envelopes; a name on no
  doorbell. *pressure:* the sketchbook at 9418 at odd hours; mail addressed

- **S5 (Carmen's exposure)** — *whisper:* the stoop occupied like a claim;

  secret-holder *lives their life* (Victor at his counter, Carmen on her
  stoop) is always stronger direction than the same tell staged in a
  neutral space. Home venues first; borrowed venues are for echoes.

## 31. Fuse interference matrix

§8's compounding exception says interlocked fuses may share a pressured
window. This matrix makes "interlocked" auditable: every unordered fuse
pair carries exactly one relation, and the relation is a *permission*,
never a summons. `drama.json` mirrors it as `fuse_interference`.

|---|---|---|
| `interlocked` | The same event read from different sides — the pair may share a pressured window (compounding, not stacking) |
| `adjacent` | Shared characters or venues — one fuse's pressure may *echo* as texture in the other's spaces, but the echo spends no budget and pressuring both is a stacked week |
| `independent` | No shared mechanics — co-pressuring is always a stacked week; shed one |
| `masked` | During the louder fuse's surface window, the masked fuse's display ceiling drops one rung (brink→pressure, pressure→whisper) — its shadows get *read through* the bigger story, which is realistic, not wasted |

| F3·F6 | adjacent | Jules's outsider narration grazes the blog mystery |

Standing rules:

- **Echoes are free, pressure costs.** An adjacent fuse's texture may show
  up inside another's pressured window; it just may not consume budget or
  advance a state. If an echo starts reading as a second pressured fuse,
  the matrix was mislabeled — fix the label at drift review, don't shed
  the echo retroactively.
- **Masking is directional and time-boxed.** It applies only while the
  louder fuse is `surfaced`-window hot (the 2–4 block days around the
  surface event), then lifts on its own. Nobody unmasks a seed by effort.
- **The matrix is a ceiling, not a quota.** An interlocked pair sharing a
  window is *permitted*, never owed. Most pressured windows stay single.

## 32. Audience suspicion calibration

§28 grades what shadows may show; this section grades what the audience
may *conclude* from them. Each rung carries a **suspicion ceiling** — the
most a careful viewer is allowed to be sure of. A shadow that would let
the audience *state the fact* (not suspect it — state it) exceeds its
rung no matter how subtle it looked on paper. `drama.json` mirrors it as
`suspicion_calibration`.

| Seed | Whisper ceiling | Pressure ceiling | Brink ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | "Victor seems off lately" | "something is up with the buildings or the landlord" | "the two Guerrero buildings specifically are in play" — never "a written offer exists" |
| S2 | "the blog is local, someone who knows the block" | "the blogger might be someone we watch" | "Mars is holding a post back" — never certainty she *is* Unfiltered |
| S3 | "Dani's art has a recurring figure" | "Dani and Marcus orbit each other" | "they're involved, and Priya doesn't know" — the lit end allows a nearer brink, never duration or seriousness |
| S4 | "Jules's paperwork is odd" | "Jules might not be on the lease" | "Jules's tenancy couldn't survive scrutiny" — never "unpermitted" named as fact |
| S5 | "Carmen is deeply rooted here" | "Carmen is worried about something she won't say" | "the worry is housing-shaped" — never the income or the nowhere-to-go |
| S6 | "Tomás has a plan" | "the plan has a name and a price" | "money moved between Tomás and Victor" — never the amount, never the handshake's terms |
| S7 | "the 3 p.m. coffee is a fixture" | "Tomás's attention is specific" | "the audience may be surer than Marisol" — the ceiling never includes *her* seeing it |
| S8 | "Jules draws a recurring figure" | "the figure is Priya; Jules lingers" | "the audience names the crush before the block does" — ahead-of-character is the design here, not a leak |
| S9 | "Marcus is generous and thin" | "money is tight at 9457" | "the arrears are ledger-real" — never a figure |
| S10 | "Priya does the shared labor" | "Priya is keeping count" | "the tally is about to be spoken aloud" — never a guarantee it is |

Standing rules:

- **The rewatch test.** Knowing everything, a viewer rewatching a rung's
  shadows should find them *fair* — present but honestly ambiguous. A
  shadow that only makes sense in hindsight is fine; a shadow that only
  makes sense because the renderer knew the secret is a leak.
- **Ceilings are per-rung and cumulative.** A brink window may still show
  whisper and pressure tells; it may not show a shadow that collapses the
  distance between "fairly sure" and "certain." The surface event does
  the certainty.
- **S8 is the calibrated exception.** Jules's crush is *designed* to run
  ahead of the characters — its ceilings are higher at every rung and
  that's correct, not a leak to fix.
- **Suspicion is measured in the audience, not the block.** A brink tell
  the characters shrug off is still a brink tell; calibration is about
  what the *viewer* can hold, since the viewer is the product.

## 33. Comedy duty — the contrast roster

§2 makes comedy load-bearing; this section makes it *accountable*. Comedy
is a standing condition of the characters, never a beat the pacing layer
may schedule — but its **absence is observable**, and that's the knob.

- **The drought flag.** A pressured window that produced zero comic
  texture anywhere on the block gets flagged `comedy_drought` at drift
  review (§22 step list, implicitly — see `drama.json`). The response is
  never "add jokes": it's a posture note preferring venues/characters
  whose ordinary texture is comic (the counter, the benches, the
  supplier loop's earnestness) over the next window. The drought flag is
  a review artifact, never a world event.
- **Comedy lands next to the shadow, never on it.** A joke that deflates

## 34. The residue nursery

§15 says resolved fuses leave residue; §25 says residue becomes the next
fuse map's raw material at a declared season boundary. The nursery is the
holding pen between those two facts — a list of *candidate seeds* that
residue is gesturing at. `drama.json` mirrors it as `residue_nursery`.

Rules:

- **Dormant-only, boundary-only.** A nursery candidate can never be
  pressured; it isn't a seed yet. Promotion to the seed registry happens
  only at a declared season boundary (§25) and only when the candidate's
  underlying fact is authored into a bible — the nursery names
  tendencies, the bibles own facts.
- **No mid-arc minting.** A fuse must be `resolved` before its residue
  can nominate a candidate. Nothing enters the nursery while its parent
  is still burning.
- **Residue seeds inherit no secret by default.** A candidate grown from
  residue describes a *new situation* (a habit, a monument, a changed
  ritual) — if it needs a held-secret core, that secret is authored at
  promotion in the bible, never backfilled.
- **Internal vocabulary.** Nursery candidates never appear on any
  spectator surface, same rule as the seed registry itself.
