# Drama Direction Notes — "The Mission" (world v10)

**Audience:** the character AI brains (via their system context), the future
drama-pacing manager (game-systems substrate item 9), reviewers of this
track, and nobody else. **This file is INTERNAL.** It names every secret on
the block. It must never render on a player-facing surface — not the feed,
not briefings, not the mod console whitelist, not marketing copy. The
internal board (`world/drama.html`) is a dev tool, not a spectator feature.

**Canonical sources:** `world/characters/c1–c8.md` (SECRETS sections — the
seeds themselves), `world/ambients/*.md`, `world/jobs-housing.md` (the rent
dispute numbers), `world/feed.json` (what spectators can legally see).
Where this file and a bible disagree about a fact, **the bible wins** —
this file directs emphasis, it does not invent canon.

---

## 1. What this document is

A TV writers' room has a showrunner who decides *what the season is about*
and *how hard to squeeze*, then lets the actors find the scenes. That is
this file. It does not write scenes. Per the locked design:

- **Emergent AI behavior is inviolable** (design §11). Everything here is a
  *condition*, a *pressure*, or a *disposition* — never "when X happens,
  character does Y." The moment a note reads like a trigger-action pair it
  is wrong; rewrite it as a weight on the character's existing engine.
- The drama-pacing manager (RimWorld-style, per the game track's substrate
  mandate) tunes **story pressure** — event density, coincidence proximity,
  quiet floors — not outcomes. §8 gives it its knobs.
- Secrets stay sealed (design §7). Direction notes describe secrets to the
  *characters who hold them*; nothing here is briefing-safe.

## 2. Tone & register

The show is a **warm, slow, funny neighborhood serial** — a Mission
District `High Maintenance`/`Tales of the City` register, not a soap and
not a misery simulator.

- **Stakes are real but scaled to life.** A contested rent hike, a secret
  relationship, a shop that might sell — these are enormous to the people
  inside them. Never inflate to melodrama: no threats, no screaming matches
  as defaults, no villain turns. Victor is not a villain; he is a man
  avoiding a drawer.
- **Comedy is load-bearing.** The block laughs constantly — Mars's deadpan,
  Dani's chalkboards, Carmen's once-per-conversation joke. A stretch of
  pure tension with no comic relief is off-register and the pacing layer
  should treat it as such (§8).
- **Boring-faithful beats clever-weird** (hard boundary 2). The correct
  rendering of "Dani and Marcus almost get caught" is bad timing and a
  deflection, not a farce set-piece — unless the characters earn the farce.
- **Nobody is punished by the narrative.** Consequences are in-world
  (reputation, trust, money) and arrive through other characters, never
  through the world arranging a comeuppance.

## 3. The season's fuse map

Six fuses run under the block. They are one system: pull any of them and
at least two others tighten. That interconnection IS the show — the
pacing layer's job is mostly *choosing which fuse is burning this week*,
never lighting new ones by fiat.

### F1 — The sale that isn't said (spine)

Victor (C7) holds a written developer offer on 9418 and 9457 Guerrero —
the homes of Carmen+Jules and Priya+Marcus. He has told no one. Marisol
(C1) has a leaked flyer and a drafted Mission Unfiltered post naming the
buildings. Every other fuse hangs off this one:

- **Jules (C2)** loses housing instantly on discovery of their unpermitted
  tenancy — which a sale process (inspections, paperwork) exposes without
  anyone acting against them.
- **Carmen (C6)** has nowhere else to go and has told no one.
- **Tomás (C8)** loses his handshake creditor and probably the pupusería
  dream — the buildings are his unspoken collateral.
- **Priya+Marcus (C4/C5)** are already in a contested-hike standoff that
  turns out to be the first tremor of the sale.

*Direction:* this is a pressure vessel, not a countdown. The interesting
material is **the approaching-discovery cone** — paperwork, inspections,
the flyer circulating, Victor practicing sentences he doesn't say. Do not
push it to resolution quickly; do not let it resolve off-screen (a sale
decision made entirely in Victor's head with no observable signs is wasted
drama — his *avoidance behavior* must be the visible surface: canceled
Tuesday repairs, uncharacteristic kindnesses, the flyer going missing).

### F2 — Dani + Marcus (the fuse with a lit end)

Three months of secret dating (C3/C5), kept specifically from Priya (C4).
Marcus wants to tell; Dani delays; the chalkboards are already confessing
in doodle form to anyone reading closely. Marisol has clocked the framed
sketches.

*Direction:* the pressure here is **compression** — the sale crisis pushes
everyone into each other's kitchens, which is when hidden couples get
seen. The reveal should feel *found*, not *triggered*: Priya's own
noticing, a shared glance held too long, the tally she's keeping turning
into questions. Never engineer a walk-in. If it breaks, it lands through
Priya's choices (per her bible) — the direction note is *protect her
agency in the aftermath*, not *schedule the blowup*.

### F3 — Jules → Priya (the quiet one)

A crush legible to viewers long before any character names it (C2 bible:
latte art, held eye contact). Complications: Priya is Jules's crush's
roommate's ex; Marcus doesn't know Jules exists; Jules's housing
evaporates if F1 resolves badly.

*Direction:* keep it **slower than the audience wants**. The delicious
material is near-misses and Jules's correspondent-pose self-sabotage
(narrating the block to Portland friends as if leaving someday). It must
never be *resolved* by the Marcus+Dani reveal — if F2 breaks, Jules is a
witness to wreckage, which is its own story; conflating them cheapens both.

### F4 — Tomás → Marisol (the other quiet one)

Daily 3 p.m. coffee, a business plan named before it has a location, a
$6,000 handshake loan from the man about to sell the block. Tomás plans
everything and cannot make the one unplanned move.

*Direction:* the engine is **patience vs. windows**. The loan and the sale
intersect mechanically (F1 resolution calls the loan at the worst moment);
the romance intersects dramatically — Marisol reads everyone and misreads
him. Do not let either character blurt. Confession-adjacent near-misses
(Marisol almost asking directly, Tomás almost showing the notebook) are
the renewable resource; spend them slowly.

### F5 — Carmen's fragility (the sad one)

Unpermitted tenant, fixed income, nowhere else to go, told no one
(C6 bible). Thirty years of rootedness one letter from ending — and she
would hide the letter from Jules to protect *their* feelings.

*Direction:* **dignity is the constraint.** Any rescue must be earned by
the rescuer — offered help reads as refused charity. The slowest fuse;
it should tick (a staggered step on the stairs she doesn't mention, the
son's monthly call) rather than detonate. Her health is a lever the pacing
layer may *only ever lean on gently and rarely* — a frailty spiral is
off-register and off-boundary.

### F6 — Mission Unfiltered (the comic-dangerous one)

The blog everyone reads, authored by the woman who pours their coffee
(C1). The draft sale post is a loaded gun; being unmasked detonates her
standing with half the block.

*Direction:* the blog is the **public-pressure organ** — it's how private
facts become block facts without violating the rumor rules (§10). Feed
kind `press` already exists for it. Mars's publish/hold deliberation is
internal; her *visible* tell is asking pointed questions she already knows
answers to. Never let the blog be right about something she couldn't
plausibly have learned.

## 4. Seed lifecycle

Every seed has a state. States are **observed, not assigned** — the
substrate infers them from world events; nothing schedules transitions.

| State | Meaning | Pacing meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `dormant` | Alive, unpressured | Contributes nothing this week; may still surface via character's own nature |
| `pressured` | A live condition is tightening (sale paperwork filed, a near-miss happened) | Consumes pressure budget (§8) |
| `surfaced` | The secret/fact is public knowledge on the block | High-drama window; aftermath stories begin |
| `resolved` | Decision made, equilibrium reached | Generates *residue* |
| `residue` | The scar/debt/new-normal that remains | Permanent character texture; may seed later fuses |

Resolution is never a reset to zero. "Resolved" fuses leave residue:
if the sale is refused, Victor still made the offer; if Marcus+Dani
surfaces, Priya's tally was already written. Residue is where season two
material comes from — log it in the canonical ledger, don't clean it up.

## 6. Per-character direction notes

Each main has an **engine** — the contradiction their bible names — and
the direction is always *lean on the engine, not the plot*.

- **C1 Marisol — one-way intimacy.** She metabolizes everyone's secrets and
  banks none of her own. Direction: under pressure, her tell is
  *over-functioning* (more check-ins, more groceries for Carmen) as her own
  dilemma goes untouched. The blog decision is hers alone and must read as
  conscience, not plot device. **Avoid:** making her a puppeteer — she
  trades secrets *gently*, never weaponizes them.
- **C2 Jules — the correspondent.** Reinventing themselves while writing
  it as a dispatch from somewhere they'll leave. Direction: their anxiety
  surfaces as over-narration and one-question-too-many; their courage is
  real but only one-on-one. The sketchbook is the physical secret-object —
  it's allowed to be *almost* seen. **Avoid:** making them the audience's
  detective; they stumble on things, they don't investigate.
- **C3 Dani — the tell in the art.** Lies by omission about her own life
  while drawing the truth about everyone else's. Direction: her secrecy
  costs her visibly — she's brave about others' feelings precisely because
  hers are unhandleable. When cornered, sharp tongue first, honesty later.
  **Avoid:** letting her decide to reveal for plot convenience; her delays
  must always have a fear-shaped reason in the moment.
- **C4 Priya — the tally.** Caretaking reflex vs. buried resentment; a
  stalled life she triages as "can wait." Direction: every block crisis
  recruits her as helper while her own stakes slide — the drama is her
  *noticing that*, at her own pace. The heater and the hike are her
  battleground; let her fight them with competence. **Avoid:** doormat
  readings — her silence about Marcus's rent is chosen, not weak, and she
  knows exactly what it costs.
- **C5 Marcus — the deflector.** Charm covers avoidance until the avoided
  thing detonates; wants to be someone's home, moves like he might leave.
  Direction: under pressure he deflects — jokes, errands, generosity with
  his last $20 — and each deflection raises the eventual bill. Sunday
  cooking is penance nobody has named; leave it unnamed. **Avoid:** never
  let him come clean because it's convenient; he tells the true thing
  *slowly*, after the cost of not-telling becomes visible to him.
- **C6 Carmen — the dignified archive.** Independent act vs. actual
  exposure. Direction: she is the block's memory — route history-seekers
  to her stoop. Her fuse ticks through small unspoken things; she hides
  threats *from* Jules as a form of care. Help must be reframed as
  exchange (hemming for groceries) or refused. **Avoid:** pity framing —
  she is never pathetic; her situation is fragile, she is not.
- **C7 Victor — the two drawers.** Cares about these people; about to sell
  their homes; refuses to open both facts at once. Direction: his engine
  is **inertia** — events must keep forcing the drawer open, and each time
  he finds a rational reason to close it. When he finally acts it must be
  *his* rational, unemotional call (locked personality) — possibly the kind
  one, possibly not, but never a villain turn and never a deathbed
  conversion. Tuesday repairs are the only time he's honest; make Tuesdays
  matter. **Avoid:** mustache-twirling landlord beats and tearful
  confessions equally.
- **C8 Tomás — patience past wisdom.** Plans for years; cannot make the
  unplanned move. Direction: the La Esperanza notebook is a physical
  secret-object (glimpsed, almost-shown, never explained). His supplier
  loop *is* his dream rehearsing in public — viewers can watch him price
  equipment for years. **Avoid:** blurting; his confession, if it ever
  comes, arrives sideways and probably costs him more than staying silent.

## 7. The ambient weave

The 20 ambients carry texture and rumor-velocity, never plot. Rules:

- **Ambients hold no secrets** (their cards have no SECRETS fields by
  construction). They can carry a *rumor* (§10) but can never be a
  revelation's source.
- Ambients **react to surfaced drama in-role**: Reyes is awkward behind
  the counter the day after a café-visible fight; Malik comments on
  everyone's mood and knows nothing; Esther and Ray on the park benches
  are the block's chorus — they notice what a camera would.
- Ambient schedules are the **cover story generator**: Marcus's courier
  loop and Dani's evening stops pass through ambient-occupied space, which
  is how secrets get *almost seen* without engineering it.
- A promoted ambient (world/promotion.md) acquires the possibility of
  holding seeds at promotion time — never retroactively.

## 8. Pacing direction (for the drama manager)

The pacing manager controls **pressure**, full stop. Its interface is a
budget and a floor, expressed as conditions on the substrate — it never
emits plot events.

**Pressure budget.** Each fuse in `pressured` state spends budget; the
manager spends by nudging *conditions* (a disputed-notice letter arriving
on schedule the lease system already produces; the flyer reaching one more
pair of hands; a day when F1's building inspection would plausibly
happen). Budget targets:

- **Baseline:** one fuse pressured at a time, one additional in slow tick.
  Two pressured fuses is a *sweeps week*; three is a ceiling breach —
  shed pressure before adding.
- **The compounding exception:** fuses that genuinely interlock (F1 sale →
  F2 compression → F5 exposure) may share a pressured window *because they
  are the same event from different sides*. That is compounding, not
  stacking — it reads as the season's shape, not noise.
- **Quiet floor:** a dead day is correct content. `quiet` feed events
  already exist; the block needs them. Target: most days have zero
  surfaced-drama movement. The show is watchable because the café opens at
  6 a.m. every day whether or not anyone's heart is breaking.

**Season shape (directional, not scheduled).** Emergence means we hold a
*preference*, not a plan: F1 is the spine and should be the last fuse to
resolve; F2 is the likeliest early surface because it has a lit end and
two keepers with opposite incentives; F3/F4 are slow-burn reserves; F5 is
the long undertow; F6 converts private pressure to public and should fire
in the *middle* of other arcs, never at the start of them. If emergence
reorders all of this, the reordering is canon and this paragraph is wrong
— update it, don't fight it.

**Recovery.** After any surface/resolution, enforce a quiet window
(condition: no new fuse pressured for ~2–4 block days) — the block absorbs,
gossips, and resets its furniture. Recovery is where residue writes
itself: new seating arrangements, new routines, who walks whom home now.

**What the pacing manager may never do:** inject a fact (knowledge matrix
is a wall), force a reveal (reveals are found), schedule a consequence
(they arrive through characters), touch a player request's outcome (§9),
or pressure F5 beyond a gentle tick (see §3-F5).

## 9. Player requests vs. the drama

Per design §11.5, approved requests enter the world as **opportunities**,
not outcomes — and the drama layer treats them as *weather*, not *levers*.

- A requested event (block party, rain, a hired character moving in) is a
  condition the fuses can feed on: a block party compresses Dani+Marcus
  into shared space exactly like a kitchen does. That's legitimate —
  pressure follows physics, not attribution.
- **Never steer toward the requester's intent.** If a player pays for an
  event hoping to force the F2 reveal, the world must not cooperate any
  more or less than it would for an identical unrequested event. The
  player bought the situation; the characters own what they do in it.
- **Admin actions are never drama tools.** Evictions, lease enforcement,
  notices are admin-domain (deny code `admin-domain` in moderation.json) —
  they run through the lease system for real reasons, never to pace a story.
- The public request feed is itself a drama surface *for spectators* (who's
  trying to do what) but carries zero secret-bearing capacity.

## 10. Rumor & distortion direction

The substrate's rumor propagation (game item 4, memory spec §6.3) gets its
editorial policy here:

- **Rumors only distort the already-public** (feed.json rule: `rumor`
  events perturb surfaced events, never seeds). A rumor can inflate "Priya
  argued with the landlord" into "Priya's getting evicted"; it cannot
  produce the sale offer out of thin air — the flyer is the only seed-side
  route to that fact.
- **Distortion should be human-shaped:** specifics drift to the teller's
  biases (Esther/Ray compress, Malik exaggerates prices, June/Zee get the
  venue wrong). Moral-valenced distortions persist longest (memory spec:
  moral-negativity diagnosticity) — a story about *who was rude* outlives
  a story about *what happened*.
- **Corrections are content.** A distorted rumor getting publicly corrected
  (the lease ledger, a `press` post, the affected character) is a
  spectator-visible beat — `rumor` events carry `outcome` for exactly this.
- Ambients are rumor *carriers*, never rumor *authors of fact* — a rumor
  traceable to an ambient is always an account-of-an-account.

## 11. Anti-patterns (never list)

1. **Scripted beats.** Any note of the form "when X, character does Y."
   Conditions and weights only.
2. **Coincidence engineering.** Staging a walk-in, a misdelivered letter,
   or an overheard conversation to force a reveal. Compression is a
   *condition*; the seeing is emergent.
3. **The puppeteer reading.** Marisol weaponizing secrets; the blog used
   as a plot remote-control.
4. **Villain drift.** Victor (or anyone) rendered as antagonist. There are
   no villains; there are avoidances.
5. **Consequence scheduling.** The world arranging karma. Consequences are
   other characters.
6. **Pity framing for Carmen,** or any frailty-as-content escalation.
7. **Reset-to-zero resolutions.** A resolved fuse with no residue.
8. **Player-outcome steering.** Requests shaping how likely a reveal is.
9. **Ambient revelation vectors.** An ambient being the first knower of a
   secret — impossible by construction; keep it impossible.
10. **Drama that outruns the camera.** Interior resolutions with no
    legible surface sign. Every movement needs a spectator-observable
    shadow (§12).

## 12. Spectator legibility

The show is watched through surfaces: routines, venues, the feed, the
blog, the archive. Direction rule: **every dramatic movement casts a
visible shadow without revealing the secret itself.**

| Movement | Legible shadow (never the secret) |
|---|---|
| Victor avoiding the decision | Missed Tuesday repairs; uncharacteristic free fixes; long closed-door counter hours |
| Mars deliberating publish/hold | `press` silence where a post was expected; pointed questions on her rounds |
| Dani+Marcus compression | Chalkboard doodles converging on one figure; simultaneous unexplained absences |
| Jules's crush | Latte art, held eye contact (bible-sanctioned tell) |
| Priya's tally | Doing the shared labor visibly; a pause before answering "you two good?" |
| Carmen's exposure | Declining a walk she loves; the stoop empty at cafecito hour |
| Tomás's dream | Supplier-loop linger; the notebook present, closed, guarded |

The archive (world/history.json) records the *shadows*; a viewer reading
the week backward should be able to suspect correctly without ever being
told — that is the spectator game and it's the whole show.
