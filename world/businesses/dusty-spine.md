# The Dusty Spine — street venue

**Code keys:** `Dog Eared Books` (parody of the real used-book shop —
code key only, never rendered) · **Corner:** Valencia at 20th side ·
**Hours:** M–F 11:00–19:00, Sat–Sun 10:00–20:00 · **Interior:** no ·
**Card type:** venue, tier `street`

A used-book shop that smells like dust and rain jackets. Narrow aisles,
a spinning YA rack by the window, a free box on the sidewalk that
empties by lunch, and a cat-shaped absence — there's a water bowl and
a sleeping ledge but the cat is never in frame when the camera looks.
Store credit is the house currency: bring a bag, leave with a smaller
bag and a paper slip with the clerk's handwriting on it.

## Rhythm

- **11:00–14:00** — browsers and the free-box crowd. Vera comes on her
  day off and alphabetizes the free box without being asked; the clerk
  pretends not to notice because the shelf looks better after.
- **15:00–17:00** — June's window. School's out, and the YA spin rack
  by the front is hers until her bus comes. Zee trails in sometimes
  and pretends she's just charging her phone.
- **Weekends** — couples doing the Valencia twenty-dollar loop:
  Dusty Spine, Marooned Records, one shared pastry. Carmen reads the
  staff-pick shelf like a menu and buys nothing, which the clerk reads
  as respect.

## Prices (game $)

Mass-market paperback $6 · trade paperback $9 · staff-pick hardcover
$18 · free box $0 (the honor system is the store's whole religion) ·
store credit: ~40% of resale, slip never expires.

## Spectator hooks (conditions, never scripts)

- What June checks out vs what she buys is a readable difference —
  borrowed books are ambitions, bought ones are keepers.
- The staff-pick shelf rotates; a new name appearing in the picks is
  the clerk reviewing someone in public.
- Carmen lingering over a book she's clearly already read is a tell
  regulars have learned to read.

## Request surface

Hires-capable (a counter job for a hired character fits the wage band).
Not lockable, not event-capable — author-night ambitions belong to
larger rooms.

## Boundaries

The cat is a running gag, not a storyline — never resolve it into a
plot. The free box must never carry a seeded object; it is texture,
not a delivery mechanism for drama.
