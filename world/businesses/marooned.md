# Marooned Records — street venue

**Code keys:** `Stranded Records` (parody of the real record shop —
code key only, never rendered) · **Corner:** Valencia side ·
**Hours:** M–F 11:00–19:00, Sat–Sun 11:00–20:00 · **Interior:** no ·
**Card type:** venue, tier `street`

A record shop the width of a shipping container: new releases up
front, used bins in the middle, a $2 45s crate by the door that
everyone flips and nobody buys from. The in-store turntable is the
room's mood ring — whoever's behind the counter programs the block's
afternoon, and regulars can tell who's working by what's playing.

## Rhythm

- **Weekdays** — slow and dig-heavy. Sam works through the dollar bin
  on Sundays before his park set, looking for songs worth ruining
  publicly; he buys one 45 a week, like a tithe.
- **Broke-month cycle** — Jules sells back the same three records
  every tight month and buys them back when the tips recover; the
  clerk keeps them on a hold shelf now, which is either kindness or
  a standing joke.
- **Weekends** — the second half of the Valencia twenty-dollar loop
  with The Dusty Spine. Bex browses the punk bins on smoke-break
  radius; Nadia buys exactly one record a month and it's always
  something nobody expected.

## Prices (game $)

45s bin $2 · used LP $8–$25 · new release $28 · the hold shelf $0
(house courtesy, not a policy).

## Spectator hooks (conditions, never scripts)

- The turntable is a public mood readout — a somber afternoon set
  from a normally upbeat counter clerk is legible to regulars.
- Sam's weekly 45 and Jules's sell-back cycle are both clockwork
  beats; a skipped week reads as signal, not absence.
- The listening-post headphones seat two — who shares them is
  information.

## Request surface

Event-capable in spirit (in-store sets are the obvious ask) but not
lockable — the room is too small; an "in-store set" would land as a
micro-event through the drama manager, not a venue lock.

## Boundaries

The hold shelf is a clerk courtesy, not a mechanic — no other
character gets one without the same earned history. Nobody's
sell-back transaction is ever secret content; embarrassment about it
is fine, extraction about it is not.
