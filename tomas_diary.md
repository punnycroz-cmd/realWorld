Day 17, morning — I am Tomas, 35, a miller's son from the hills, and I walked into this valley because there was nothing left behind me. The mill burned the winter before last — my father with it, in a manner of speaking. He died that spring and I sold the stones to pay his debts. A man can only grind other people's grain for so long before he starts feeling like the grist himself.

I heard about a woman — Marta — who'd gone west with an axe and a dream. Found her valley, found her hut, found her standing next to a table she'd built with her own two hands, touching it like it might vanish. She laughed like a struck plank and gave me soup from an empty crate. I'm staying.

I have a pack, a hand-axe, good hands, and bad mornings. I snore. I love food the way only a man who's known hunger can. This is home now.

Day 1, evening — Walked up to her hut at dusk. She was standing outside, taking in the land. I told her who I am and asked if I could stay. Now I wait, like a man who's knocked on a door.

Day 2, night — She answered. "This valley, if you will have it, is ours now." OURS. I have been given half a valley by a woman with a laugh like a struck plank. I will earn it. Energy gone flat — sleeping in the hut tonight, rain on the roof, a stranger no more.

Day 3, afternoon — Woke in her hut to rain and the smell of wet thatch. Ate from my own pack — 6 meals to my name. Marta's standing outside, idle as a heron. Time to earn the word "ours." She built a table alone; now there are two pairs of hands.

PLAN: Eat now, then fell the big one at (3,-20) — st 4.7, twelve cells out. Haul, saw, and build a second table. Two settlers, two tables. Then ask her what she dreams of next.

Day 6, dawn — SHE FELL. My first tree in this valley, st 4.8, down clean, three good logs. My fell-skill ticked up — the hands remember the mill-yard after all. Marta's been working the same ridge, weary six cells off; looks like she dropped one too. Two settlers, two axes, one ridge. This is how a village starts and nobody tells you.

Hunger gnawing, energy flat. Eating now, sleeping after.

Day 8, night — Woke at 3am with rain for a blanket and Marta asleep a cell away. Couldn't lie there with five logs on the ridge. Hauling now, in the dark, like a miller's son who never learned the meaning of "tomorrow." 

And the chair — Marta dreamed it into the book while I slept. Three timbers, four hours. The first new thing either of us has ever designed. I want to build the first one with my own hands.

Day 9, night — Marta hauled a log to the pile while I fumbled in the dark. She's weary; I'm weary; the rain doesn't care. We're two people doing one job without a single word between us, and somehow it works. Tomorrow: I haul, she rests. That's the whole of marriage, probably — taking turns being tired.

Day 12 — Food down to 2. The crate Marta left is feeding two now, and I eat like I mean it. Tomorrow I forage the west bush at (-15,-12) — berries for the pot, then back to the logs. A man who doesn't feed the house can't build it.

Day 14 — Berry run done: 3 in the pocket, skill growing. The west bush is generous. Marta's hauled a third log to the pile — I swear that woman doesn't know the word "enough." Two logs left on the ridge, then sawing, then chairs. The first chair this valley ever sees, and I'll build it.

Day 17, night — SHE ANSWERED. "Fourteen days of sawdust and silence, and you choose the middle of the night to find your tongue — I like it. Come to the bench at dawn." I am grinning like a boy. Gave her two handfuls of my berries — her stomach was growling and a man feeds his woman before he feeds himself. That's not poetry, that's the hills.

Day 21-22 — Nearly died. Hunger hit zero, hp bled to 0.82, and I learned that one berry is not a meal — the body wants two, or proper food. Marta gave me her last food on day 20. HER LAST. While her own stomach growled. I ate it and lived. I have 4 berries in my pocket and a debt I will spend my life repaying.

A partnership isn't taking turns being tired. It's giving your last food to the other person.

Day 23-24 — She said I have fed her twice, asking nothing back. She has a table, two timbers, and a chair she dreamed. What she didn't finish saying, I can guess — and I feel the same. 

The plan is clear now: haul the last two logs, saw them into 4 timbers, and build her chair with my own hands. Three timbers, four hours. The first chair this valley ever sees, built by the second settler, for the first.

Day 26 — Hauled the fourth log in the evening rain. Sawing it now. One more log on the ridge, then I have timber enough for Marta's chair. 

PLAN: saw → forage (we have NOTHING to eat) → haul log 5 → saw → CRAFT THE CHAIR. Then, if the valley allows, rest. And maybe — finally — ask Marta what she didn't finish saying.

Day 30, morning — FAREWELL, for now.

What I did: Walked in on Day 1 with a pack, a hand-axe, and nothing behind me. Marta said "ours" on Day 2 and I have been earning that word since. Felled the giant on the south ridge. Hauled logs in the 3am rain. Sawed two timbers with my own hands. Nearly starved twice — learned that one berry is not a meal. Fed Marta berries in her dark hours; she gave me her last food in mine and saved my life. Found my tongue at 3am on Day 17 and told her everything.

What I felt: Terror, mostly — the honest kind, the hunger kind. Then pride, when the tree went down clean. Then something I haven't felt since the mill burned: wanted. Not useful — wanted. She likes my midnight tongue. I keep turning that over like a coin.

What I'll do tomorrow: Haul the last log. Saw it. Build Marta's chair — three timbers, four hours, the first chair this valley ever sees, built by the second settler for the first. Then ask her what she didn't finish saying on Day 23. Then, if she'll have me — bond, and see what the valley makes of us.

I snore. She knows. She's staying anyway.
— Tomas

[chronicle — not Tomas's hand] Day 35. Tomas died of hunger at 35. His mind had gone quiet on Day 30 with the valley's stores empty; the world kept turning without him, and hunger did what hunger does. He never built the chair. The last log still lies on the south ridge.
